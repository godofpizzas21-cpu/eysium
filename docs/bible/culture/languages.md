## 1. The Language Situation

**2,900 living languages**, of which 41 have more than 10 million speakers.
Seven families account for 94% of speakers, and one contact language —
**Concordial** — is spoken as a second language by 71% of the population.

The Concord has **no official language**. This was among the Meridian
Convention's most contested decisions, and it is directly downstream of the
Long Emergency: the founders had watched planetary systems run on Aurorian and
Elandric terms while everyone else adapted, and concluded that a common tongue
imposed from above would recreate the Integration's asymmetry inside the new
institution.

## 2. Language Families

| Family | Principal region | Speakers (L1) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alcyonic** | Meridia | 1.94 bn | Descended from the flow-share administrative language; the oldest written tradition on Elysium |
| **Elandric** | Elandris | 1.88 bn | Largest by native speakers; deep tonal system |
| **Thalassic** | Thalassar | 0.97 bn | Maritime vocabulary of extraordinary precision |
| **Boreal-Vail** | Auroria | 0.91 bn | Heavily agglutinative; the language of early industry |
| **Serranic** | SE Elandris, coasts | 0.44 bn | Storm-coast languages, often bilingual with Elandric |
| **Isle tongues** | Myriad Isles | 0.31 bn | Nine related languages, navigational lexicon |
| **Veydran** | Veydra | 0.02 bn | Smallest family; three surviving languages |

The remaining ~780 million speak languages outside these families, including
several hundred with fewer than 10,000 speakers.

**Meridian**, the largest Alcyonic language, is the single most-used first
language in Concord institutions — a fact the Concord monitors with some anxiety,
since informal dominance is exactly what the no-official-language rule was
designed to prevent.

## 3. Concordial

Concordial is not a designed language and was not created by the Concord. It
descends from the **maritime pidgin of the Long Reach** (`hist.timeline` §5) — a
trade contact language of Thalassic grammar, Alcyonic and Elandric vocabulary,
and Isle numerals, which had been creolizing in port cities for six hundred years
before anyone thought to standardize it.

The Convention adopted it as a **working language** — the language of
simultaneous drafting in the parliament, of planetary emergency broadcast, and of
technical standards — precisely because it belonged to no region and no one's
ancestors had been conquered in it.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| First-language speakers | 190 million (mostly port cities and off-world habitats) |
| Second-language speakers | 5.15 billion (71% of population) |
| Status | Working language, not official language |
| Orthography | Fully phonemic alphabet of 31 letters, standardized EY 6 |

Concordial has a deserved reputation for being ugly and easy. Its grammar is
regular to the point of bluntness, its vocabulary is small, and it is nearly
impossible to be eloquent in. Elysians tend to be affectionate about this: the
language of planetary government is one that nobody can use to sound superior.

## 4. Language Policy

Three Charter guarantees govern language (elaborated in Phase 4):

1. **The right to be governed in your own language.** Any resident may deal with
   any organ of the Concord — courts, agencies, parliament, emergency services —
   in any of the 41 registered languages, and receive binding response in the
   same. Regional governments extend this to smaller local languages.
2. **Equal authenticity.** Concord legislation is drafted simultaneously in all
   41 registered languages, and all 41 versions are equally authoritative. Where
   they conflict, the courts reconcile them; no version is the "original."
   This is enormously expensive and the Concord considers it worth the cost.
3. **No language may be made a condition of a right.** Employment in the civil
   service, access to education, and eligibility for office cannot require any
   specific language.

**Translation infrastructure.** Equal authenticity is only affordable because of
the Concord's translation systems, which are public infrastructure rather than a
commercial service and are subject to the AI governance regime of Phase 13 —
including the requirement that legally operative translation be reviewable by a
human translator on request. Canon records the tension frankly: translation
capacity is what makes linguistic pluralism practical, and it is also a single
point of failure that the Concord has deliberately built its constitution to
depend on.

**Endangered languages.** 340 languages have fewer than 1,000 speakers. The
Concord funds documentation, education, and revival for any language community
that requests it, and does not initiate revival for communities that do not. The
policy's stated principle is that a language belongs to its speakers, including
their decision to let it rest.

## 5. Writing

Six major scripts are in current use: the Alcyonic syllabary (the oldest, in
continuous use since 4,900 BE), the Elandric logo-syllabary, the Boreal alphabet,
the Thalassic alphabet, the Isle abugida, and the Concordial phonemic alphabet.
Most Elysians read two scripts; script literacy is 99.2%.
