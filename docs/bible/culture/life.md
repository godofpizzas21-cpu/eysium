## 1. The Festival Year

Two observances are universal and were canonized in Phase 3B: **the Naming** on
Aumar 1, austere and local, at which communities read their own dead from the
Long Emergency; and **Thresholdday**, the intercalary day, frivolous and without
obligation (`cult.foundations` §3).

Everything else is regional, and the Concord funds none of it.

| Festival | Where | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **The Turning** | Planet-wide, Verane 1 | Year's start at the northward equinox; observed everywhere and differently everywhere |
| **Kalyra Nights** | Coastal Regions | Marked on the major moon's cycle; tide festivals, night markets, and swimming |
| **The Long Dark** | Auroria | Six weeks of the deep winter; lantern processions, communal cooking, and the slow songs |
| **Onset** | Elandris | The monsoon's arrival, dated by forecast rather than calendar and therefore never on the same day twice |
| **The Reading of Waters** | Alcyon basin | Publication of the year's flow-share allocation, now a festival that was once an administrative act |
| **Aurorafall** | Mid-latitudes | Informal; people go outside when the aurora is strong, and cities dim their lighting further to let them |

**The Reading of Waters** is the one Elysians find most characteristic of
themselves: a bureaucratic publication, eight and a half thousand years old, that
became a celebration.

## 2. Sport

61% of adult Elysians play organised sport, and interregional competition is
intense in a way that Elysian politics deliberately is not — regional pride
without nationalism (`cult.foundations` §5) finds most of its outlet here.

**Cassel** is the planetary game: eight players a side, continuous play, no
offside, a low-bouncing ball handled with both hands. The eight-a-side structure
comes from the week, and the six-digit grip makes one-handed catching and
throwing natural in a way it would not be for a five-digit species. Every Region
has a league; the interregional season runs across the second half of the year.

Other major forms: **open-water distance swimming** in the Isles, **cliff
running** on the Thalassar Rim, **the Circuit** (a multi-stage cycling race that
crosses a different continent each year), and traditional wrestling forms in
Auroria and Veydra.

**The enhancement problem is unresolved and interesting.** Somatic gene therapy
is routine medicine on Elysium (`health.practice` §4), which makes the Earth
concept of a clean line between treatment and enhancement unworkable. Elysian
sport handles it by declaring categories rather than prohibitions: competitions
state which interventions are within their category, athletes disclose fully, and
disclosure failure — not intervention — is the offence. Canon records that this
works adequately and satisfies nobody.

## 3. Food

Elysian food culture is regional, cooperative, and unremarkable in the way that
matters: cultured and fermented protein is ordinary food
(`agri.production` §6), most living Elysians have eaten nothing else in that
category, and the historical argument about whether it counts as real food is
studied in schools as a curiosity.

The daily structure follows biphasic sleep. The main meal is eaten in **late
second waking**, well into the long evening, and is social by default — the
Seldan hearth-day meal with several generations at the table is the archetypal
Elysian domestic scene (`hist.demographics` §4). The Stillness is preceded by a
small, light meal, and Elysians regard eating heavily before it as a mild
personal failing.

Regional cuisines are strong and distinct, cooperative food processing dominates
(`econ.markets` §1), and 19% of adults are enrolled in some form of cooking or
food-preservation learning at any time (`edu.lifelong` §3).

## 4. Heritage

**The doctrine is maintained ruin, not reconstruction.**

**Ilvaret** — the first city, whose collapse in 1,900 BE is the formative memory
of Elysian culture (`hist.timeline` §4) — is stabilised and excavated and has
never been rebuilt. Its walls are held up; its gaps are left. New Ilvaret sits
beside it, deliberately separate. The Concord's heritage service states the
principle plainly: a reconstruction is a claim about what was there, and a ruin
is a record of not knowing.

The **Meridian Stone** on the Alcyon estuary, where the Convention sat, is
similarly unadorned — an unenclosed marker in an open field, with the Assembly's
purpose-built city of Sennary a few kilometres away and out of sight of it
(`city.urbanism` §2).

**Repatriation.** The Long Reach (`hist.timeline` §5) moved enormous quantities
of material out of the Isles and the southern continents into Thalassari and
later Aurorian collections. The Concord's repatriation programme, running since
EY 44, has returned 2.1 million objects. Roughly 180,000 remain contested, mostly
where the originating community no longer exists as an identifiable body. Those
are held in trust, publicly listed as contested, and displayed with their history
of acquisition rather than without it.

**Museums** number 41,000, most of them small and regional. The Record Office's
permanent archive is distributed across the library network with at least four
geographically separated copies of everything (`edu.lifelong` §4) — heritage
policy and redundancy doctrine being, on Elysium, the same policy.

## 5. Media and the Attention Problem

Canon has recorded since Phase 4B that **the Concord has solved disclosure and
not solved attention** (`gov.administration` §7). Everything is published; almost
nothing is read. This section is the Concord's attempt at an answer, and its
admission that the answer is partial.

**There is no advertising-funded mass media.** Not by prohibition — advertising
is lawful — but because the same cultural and regulatory instinct that forbids
public assistants from optimising for engagement (`ai.applications` §2) made
attention-farmed media commercially and socially marginal. What exists instead:

| Form | Share of journalism |
|---|---|
| **Levy-funded independent outlets** | 44% |
| Cooperative and reader-owned outlets | 27% |
| Stewardship-foundation outlets | 18% |
| Commercial subscription outlets | 11% |

The **media levy** is a small charge on communications infrastructure, collected
publicly and distributed by an arm's-length body that may not consider editorial
content — allocation follows audited reach and a published formula, and the
allocating body's own decisions are appealable to the Court of Review.

**Journalists have specific standing** in Elysian law: priority handling of
access requests, protected-disclosure status for sources, and a statutory right
to attend any public body's proceedings. 14% of access requests are filed by
libraries on residents' behalf (`edu.lifelong` §4); a further 31% are filed by
journalists.

**And it is still not enough.** Readership of the material that matters most —
audit reports, budget statements, environmental accounts, the Negative
Register — remains low, and the Concord's own reviews say so. The three
mechanisms it has built (libraries, funded journalism, and the Contention
tradition) improve the position and do not fix it. Canon states the residual
plainly: **a right of access to record is worth what someone's willingness to read
the record is worth, and no constitution can compel that.**

## 6. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **The attention problem persists** | Three mechanisms, real improvement, and readership of the material that matters most remains low. The Concord has no fourth idea |
| **Enhancement categories satisfy nobody** | Disclosure-based sport categories work adequately and are disputed at every interregional season |
| **Contested repatriation** | 180,000 objects with no identifiable originating community; held in trust, which is a holding position rather than a resolution |
| **Levy allocation by reach** | A formula following audited reach rewards outlets that are already read, which is precisely the dynamic the levy was meant to counter |
| **Festival funding is unequal** | The Concord funds no festivals, so regional cultural life tracks regional fiscal capacity |
| **Small-language culture is fragile** | 340 languages have under 1,000 speakers; documentation is funded on request and several communities have not requested it |
