# Cultural Foundations

**Document ID:** `cult.foundations`
**Status:** Proposed
**Version:** 1.2.0
**Inherits:** `hist.timeline`, `hist.demographics`, `cult.languages`

This chapter establishes the **substrate** of Elysian culture: shared values,
belief and its absence, remembrance, and the rhythms of ordinary life. The
expressive culture built on top of it — museums, arts, music, theatre,
architecture, festivals, sports, heritage — is owned by **Phase 14** and is not
pre-empted here.

---

## 1. The Five Civic Virtues

Every Elysian child learns five words. They are not a state ideology — the
Charter forbids compulsory belief — but they are the vocabulary in which Elysians
argue about how to live, and they appear in constitutional preambles, school
curricula, engineering codes, and ordinary insults.

**Candour.** Say what is true, especially when it is against you. The Public
Record Act (`hist.timeline` §13) is candour written into law; so is the
constitutional requirement that audits be published unedited. The Elysian
compliment *"they gave a candid account"* is high praise. The corresponding vice
is not lying but **presentation** — arranging true things to mislead.

**Stewardship.** You hold things in trust for people who cannot argue with you:
the dead, the unborn, the biosphere. Stewardship is why the carbon corridor is
constitutional rather than legislative, and why retained carbon
(`planet.resources` §7) is counted as an asset precisely because it is unused.

**Redundancy.** Anything that matters should have a second way of working. This
is an engineering principle that became a moral one. Elysians distrust elegance
that depends on nothing going wrong, and the phrase *"a single point of failure"*
is used in Elysian relationship advice as readily as in grid design.

**Restraint.** Having the capacity to do something is not a reason. The Abolition
(EY 34), the prohibition on deep-sea mining, the refusal to burn 2.9 Tt of
accessible fossil carbon, and the absence of any birth policy are all read by
Elysians as the same virtue. Restraint is the Concord's answer to the question
its founding philosophy poses: how is a civilization powerful without being
aggressive, wealthy without being cruel?

**Repair.** Nothing is finished; everything is maintained. The most admired
Elysian professions are unglamorous and continuous — the auditor, the grid
engineer, the reef technician, the archivist. The current era is literally named
the Long Maintenance, and Elysians name it that with pride rather than
resignation.

The five are taught as **being in tension with each other**, deliberately.
Candour can wound; restraint can become paralysis; redundancy is expensive and
competes with stewardship of materials. Elysian civic education is largely the
study of these conflicts, and Elysians are, as a result, an argumentative people
who are unusually comfortable disagreeing without concluding.

## 2. Belief

The Charter guarantees freedom of belief and of non-belief, establishes no
religion, and forbids any religious test for office or benefit. Religious
communities are self-governing and receive no state funding and no state
interference.

| Tradition | Share of population |
|---|---|
| No affiliation (secular, philosophical, or indifferent) | 38% |
| **Stewardship traditions** (Vesseline and related) | 21% |
| Theistic traditions (various, mostly Elandric and Aurorian) | 17% |
| **Alcyonic ancestral practice** | 14% |
| Local, syncretic, and other | 10% |

**Stewardship traditions** are the largest organised family of belief and are
mostly **non-theistic**. Their root is the Isle ocean-stewardship practice of the
Myriad Isles (`planet.geography` §2.6), which held that the sea is not owned and
that a people's worth is measured by what they leave intact. The Long Emergency
made this compelling far beyond the Isles. Vesseline practice centres on
obligation to the future rather than worship: its central observance is a
periodic public accounting of what one has taken and what one has returned.

**Alcyonic ancestral practice** is older than any of it — the continuous
lineage-and-memory tradition of the Alcyon basin, tied to the flow-share and to
the ruins of Ilvaret. It coexists comfortably with both secular and theistic
identity; a substantial share of the "no affiliation" group still keeps Alcyonic
ancestral observances, and Elysians see no contradiction in this.

**Theistic traditions** are diverse, mostly regional, and — canon should be plain
about this — have declined markedly since the founding without being suppressed.
That decline is a live source of grievance in parts of Auroria and Elandris, and
Concord politics includes a real, ongoing argument about whether a state that
funds nothing religious is genuinely neutral or merely quietly secularizing. The
Charter's answer is neutrality; the complaint that neutrality is not the same as
fairness has never gone away, and canon does not resolve it.

## 3. Remembrance

The Long Emergency killed 610 million people within living memory of Elysian
lifespans — a 130-year lifespan means that at the founding there were still
people alive who remembered the Amarant War, and that Concord history has had
only about three full lifetimes to become the past. This is the most important
single fact about modern Elysian emotional life. **The Concord is not an old
civilization remembering an old trauma. It is a young institution built by
survivors, three generations ago.**

Two universal observances mark it, and both are austere by design.

**The Naming** falls on the first day of Aumar. Communities read aloud the names
of their own dead from the Long Emergency — not planetary totals, but local
lists, which take between twenty minutes and nine hours depending on the town.
There are no speeches. Officials attend but do not speak.

**Thresholdday** (`hist.calendar` §3), the intercalary day, is the Concord's only
universal holiday and its opposite in mood: a day outside the calendar with no
work, no courts, no markets, and no scheduled governance. Its customs are
regional and mostly frivolous. Elysians describe the pairing without irony — one
day a year for the dead, one day every four years for nothing at all.

## 4. The Rhythms of Ordinary Life

Elysian daily life is shaped by biphasic sleep (`hist.demographics` §1) more than
by any cultural choice.

- **First waking** runs from dawn to early afternoon and holds most formal work,
  schooling, and institutional business.
- **The Stillness** is the early-afternoon rest period — roughly 1.5 civil hours
  in which most commerce pauses. Shops close, transport thins, and cities become
  genuinely quiet. It is protected by labour law (Phase 6), and visitors to
  Elysium find it the most striking thing about the planet.
- **Second waking** runs from mid-afternoon to late night, and is the longer of
  the two. The Elysian *evening* — the hours after the working day and before
  sleep — is unusually long, and it is where most social, family, artistic, and
  civic life happens. Elysian public spaces are designed for it (Phase 10);
  Elysian culture is largely made in it (Phase 14).

The eight-day week gives two rest days (`hist.calendar` §5): **Seldan**, the
hearth day, customarily domestic and multi-generational; and **Ovdan**, the
open day, customarily outdoors and public.

## 5. Attitudes That Recur

Five dispositions appear across Elysian regions and classes and are worth
recording, because later phases will keep needing them.

- **Comfort with disagreement.** Consensus is regarded with suspicion; a
  proposal that no one opposed is assumed to be under-examined. Concord
  procedure formalises this in mandatory minority reports.
- **Suspicion of speed.** "Quickly" is a warning word in Elysian institutional
  language. Emergencies are the recognised exception, and the Emergency of
  EY 233 is the recognised reason emergencies are watched closely.
- **Discomfort with heroism.** Elysians are wary of stories with saviours. Even
  **Teyra Oskan**, who refused the Cassian launch recommendation, is taught less
  as a hero than as evidence that the system had been built to require one —
  which is understood to be the failure. She asked that nothing be named for
  her, and nothing is (`ai.governance` §1).
- **Ease with the long term.** A species that expects 112 years takes
  century-scale plans personally. Elysians find Earth-style discount rates
  morally strange, not merely economically wrong (Phase 6).
- **Regional pride without nationalism.** Regional identity is strong,
  affectionate, and mostly expressed through food, dialect, sport, and
  argument. Territorial nationalism is culturally dead — not forbidden, but
  regarded roughly as duelling is: a thing serious people used to do.
