## 1. Physics

Elysian physics is in an era its own practitioners call **the Long Plateau**.

The standard framework of particle physics was completed around EY 270 and has
resisted extension since. There has been **no confirmed advance beyond it in 140
years**, despite the largest instruments the Concord has ever built: a 340 km
circumference collider under the Meridian Plain, a gravitational-wave
interferometer array with arms on three continents, and a neutrino observatory
beneath the Veydran ice.

Canon states this plainly because Elysian physicists do. The dominant view is
that the next scale is simply out of reach — the energies required exceed what
any instrument the Concord could build would reach — and that physics has
encountered not a failure of imagination but a wall. A vocal minority holds that
the field has been asking the wrong questions for a century and has become
institutionally incapable of asking different ones. Both positions are funded.

**Applied physics is flourishing regardless**: plasma physics for fusion,
condensed matter, metrology, and quantum information are all productive, and the
gap between a stalled fundamental frontier and a thriving applied one is a
standing theme in Elysian science writing.

## 2. Astronomy

Astronomy is unusually strong on Elysium, for reasons that are partly cultural
and partly geographic. Dark-sky standards cover **61% of land**
(`env.conservation` §4), driven as much by the cultural place of the aurora as by
astronomy itself, and Veydra offers cold, dry, stable air at altitude.

Principal facilities: the Veydran optical and infrared array; a radio observatory
on **Kalyra's far side**, permanently shielded from Elysium's radio emissions
(`space.infrastructure`); the Tyrran Belt survey network; and a wide-field exoplanet monitor.

**The search for other minds.** The Concord conducts a systematic survey for
technological signatures. It has found nothing. **Forty-one candidate signals
have been investigated since EY 96 and all forty-one have been resolved** as
natural phenomena or as Elysian instruments. Canon is deliberate about this: no
contact has occurred, the question remains open, and the Concord's first-contact
preparedness (`dipl.external` §3) is a policy exercise rather than a response to anything.

The absence is itself a live scientific problem. Helia is an ordinary star in an
ordinary galaxy, and the Concord's astronomers regard the silence as data they
cannot yet interpret.

## 3. Materials Science

The most heavily funded applied field, and the one carrying the Concord's most
consequential unmet objective.

**Substitution** is a century programme two hundred years old
(`res.system` §4), targeting the Constrained List. Its record is mixed and canon
reports it honestly:

- **Successes:** rare-earth-free motors and generators are now standard, cutting
  rare-earth demand by 71%; indium-free transparent conductors have reached
  commercial viability; several platinum-group catalysts have iron-based
  replacements.
- **The failure:** nothing replaces beryllium as a neutron multiplier. Lead-based
  multipliers work and are meaningfully worse, and no candidate has closed the
  gap in two centuries of directed effort. This is the single most important
  negative result in Elysian science.

Other active areas: engineered timber and low-carbon cement (`ind.industry` §5),
plasma-facing materials for fusion, and biopolymers replacing petrochemical
feedstock.

## 4. Biology and Genomics

Elysian biology begins from **helicin**, the six-base hereditary molecule with
4,096 codon meanings and 31 proteinogenic amino acids (`planet.biosphere` §1).
Its larger alphabet and native error correction make Elysian genomics both
richer and, in a specific way, easier than Earth genetics would be.

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Complete reference genomes | 1.06 million species |
| Described multicellular species | 2.41 million |
| Synthetic biology licences in force | 14,200 |

**Origin-of-life research** centres on the Myriad hydrothermal fields
(`planet.biosphere` §4), which host chemosynthetic ecosystems entirely
independent of sunlight and are the closest available analogue to the conditions
in which helicin arose. The fields are permanently protected and research is
non-extractive and licensed.

Synthetic biology is permitted, licensed, and contained: any organism carrying
engineered helicin must include a **dependency lock** — an engineered requirement
for a compound not present in the wild — and containment failures are reportable
band 3 offences. Eleven escapes have been recorded since EY 200; nine were
contained within a season, two established persistent populations that are still
being managed.

## 5. Medicine

Clinical practice is Phase 9's; the research frontier belongs here.

The defining fact is that **Elysian longevity is inherited, not achieved**
(`hist.demographics` §1). Median lifespan is 112 EY and maximum verified lifespan
is 139 EY, and classical-era Elysians already lived long. What Concord medicine
has done is close the gap between median and maximum — median healthy lifespan
has risen from 71 EY at the founding to 104 EY now.

**Extending the maximum has not worked.** Two centuries of directed research have
moved the verified maximum by 3 Elysian years, and the dominant view is that
helicin's error-correction capacity sets a ceiling that incremental intervention
does not reach. Research continues at modest scale and is regarded within the
field as speculative.

Productive frontiers: regenerative medicine and organ replacement, immune
engineering, antimicrobial development (a stewardship-foundation stronghold, since
the economics defeat ordinary firms), and the epidemiology of an old population —
the Concord's standing vulnerability (`hist.demographics` §6).

## 6. Neuroscience

Two Elysian peculiarities shape the field.

**Biphasic sleep.** The 26-hour circadian period and the Stillness
(`hist.demographics` §1) make Elysian sleep architecture different enough from
any single-phase system that Elysian sleep science is largely its own discipline.
Its practical outputs are substantial: the labour regulations protecting the
Stillness, shift-work health rules, and school-day design (`edu.schooling` §2) all
rest on it.

**The neural privacy prohibition.** Non-consensual neural reading or writing is
prohibited absolutely under Charter right 7, which is non-derogable. There is no
research exception, no security exception, and no emergency exception. Consensual
research is permitted with layered consent that may be withdrawn retroactively,
requiring destruction of derived data.

The prohibition genuinely constrains the field. Elysian neuroscience is
correspondingly strong on consented clinical work — neural prosthetics, spinal
and sensory restoration, epilepsy and movement disorders — and weak on the
large-scale correlational work an unconstrained field would pursue. Its
practitioners describe this as a cost they support.

Consciousness research exists, is respectable, and has produced no consensus.
Canon records that Elysians have not solved this either.

## 7. Engineering and the Applied Sciences

Engineering research is organised around the Concord's standing problems rather
than around disciplines: grid resilience and islanding, long-duration storage,
plasma-facing materials, distributed manufacturing, orbital launch economics,
disassembly and recovery, and structures with 150-year design lives.

The **Comparative Governance Register** (`gov.regions` §3) deserves mention here
as a research instrument rather than an administrative one. Thirty-four Regions
running four different governing forms, four judicial appointment methods, and
divergent curricula constitute the largest continuous natural experiment in
institutional design any civilization has run. Elysian social science is built
on it, and its most-cited finding remains the unexplained one: Assembly–Manager
Regions deliver best and are trusted least.

## 8. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **The Long Plateau** | 140 years without a fundamental advance in physics, with no agreement on whether the wall is physical or institutional |
| **Beryllium substitution** | The most important unmet objective in Elysian science, unmet after two centuries |
| **The maximum lifespan ceiling** | Two centuries of work has moved it 3 years; the field considers further effort speculative and funds it anyway |
| **Silence** | Forty-one candidate signals, forty-one resolved. The absence of detected minds is a scientific problem the Concord cannot currently address |
| **Synthetic biology escapes** | Two persistent engineered populations remain under management; dependency locks have failed in practice as well as in principle |
| **Neural privacy limits the field** | A prohibition its own practitioners support and which nonetheless leaves whole questions unaskable |
| **Consciousness unresolved** | Respectable, funded, and no closer to consensus than at the founding |
