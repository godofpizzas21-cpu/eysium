## 1. Two Services, Not One

The most consequential fact about Elysian policing is how little of it there is,
and the reason is structural: **most of what a police force would otherwise do
has been given to someone else.**

| Service | Strength | Handles |
|---|---|---|
| **Commune Response Teams** | 3.12 million (43 per 100,000) | Mental health crisis, welfare checks, intoxication, disputes without violence, missing persons, most road incidents, and the great majority of emergency calls |
| **District Police Services** | 6.38 million (88 per 100,000) | Investigation and response to crime |

Response Teams are unarmed, wear no uniform resembling police, carry no power
of arrest, and are staffed by health, social-work, and de-escalation
professionals. They are dispatched from the same emergency number as police, by
a triage system that sends police only where a crime is reported or violence is
present or credibly threatened.

**61% of emergency calls are resolved by a Response Team with no police
involvement.** The arrangement dates from the Consolidation and was not
originally a rights reform — it began as a workload measure — but it is now
defended primarily on the grounds that sending an armed institution to a
psychiatric emergency is a category error.

## 2. Structure and Doctrine

Policing is a **District competence** regulated by the Region
(`gov.regions` §1). There are 1,104 District Police Services. There is no
planetary police force. Planetary offences (`law.substantive` §1) are
investigated by a small **Concord Investigation Office** which has no patrol
function, no routine arrest powers, and must act through District services for
any physical enforcement — a deliberate refusal to create a planetary
constabulary.

Every service is organised in four branches: Response, Investigation,
Community Liaison, and a small Specialist branch.

### Arms

**Routine officers are unarmed.** Patrol and response officers carry
communications, restraints, and medical equipment. Firearms are held by
Specialist units — roughly 1.1% of officers — and deployed only on authorisation
by a duty commander of a specified rank, per incident, recorded and reviewed.

Civilian firearm ownership is licensed, requires demonstrated purpose and
periodic renewal, and stands at 2.9 per 100 households, overwhelmingly for
hunting and livestock protection in rural Regions. The Concord's low armament is
usually attributed to the Abolition and the culture of restraint
(`cult.foundations` §1); Concord criminologists are more inclined to credit the
absence of a drug trade to fight over (`law.substantive` §2.1).

### Use of force

Force must be necessary, proportionate, and the least available. Every use of
force above physical restraint — every drawn weapon, every discharge, every
restraint causing injury — is reported within 24 hours, reviewed independently,
and published in aggregate.

| Indicator | Annual value |
|---|---|
| Homicides | 29,000 (0.4 per 100,000) |
| Firearm discharges by police | 610 |
| Deaths following police contact | 148 |
| Uses of force resulting in injury | 41,300 |
| Complaints upheld against officers | 8,900 |

The 148 figure is published with the name, District, and circumstances of every
death, and every one triggers a mandatory independent investigation and a public
inquiry report. Canon states the number rather than the rate because the
Concord's own convention is that deaths at the hands of the state are counted,
not averaged.

## 3. Oversight

Every police service is subject to the Charter floor requiring an audit body it
cannot appoint, fund, or discipline (`gov.regions` §3). In practice this is the
**Police Conduct Office**, one per Region, independent of both police and the
regional executive.

- **No self-investigation.** A police service may not investigate a complaint
  against itself. The Conduct Office investigates, with its own investigators.
- **No qualified immunity.** There is no doctrine shielding officers from
  liability for rights breaches. The service is liable in damages; the officer
  faces the conduct process and, where the conduct is criminal, prosecution by
  the Prosecution Service like anyone else.
- **Recording is not held by police.** All custodial and interaction recordings
  are held by the Record Office (`law.substantive` §3), which police may
  request but not edit or delete. This single arrangement is credited by the
  Conduct Offices with more disciplinary effect than any rule.
- **Officers must intervene.** An officer who witnesses excessive force by a
  colleague and does not intervene or report commits a conduct offence of equal
  gravity to the force itself.
- **The dismissal record follows the officer.** A dismissed officer is entered
  on a planetary register and cannot be employed by any other service.

Canon is candid that oversight works unevenly. Conduct Offices in small Regions
are chronically under-resourced (`gov.regions` §7, capacity asymmetry), and
three Regions' Offices have been found by the Audit Service to be too close to
the services they oversee.

## 4. Surveillance

Surveillance is the sharpest live tension in Elysian public law, because right 7
(privacy) is non-derogable and effective policing wants data.

The settlement:

- **Judicial warrant for everything.** Interception, location tracking, covert
  entry, and access to communications data all require prior judicial
  authorisation, particularised to a person and a purpose. There is no
  administrative authorisation and no emergency bypass beyond 12 hours, which
  must then be ratified or the material destroyed.
- **No bulk collection.** Untargeted collection of communications or movement
  data is prohibited outright, not merely regulated. The prohibition survived
  three attempts to relax it, the most recent in EY 388.
- **Notification.** Every person subject to covert surveillance is notified once
  the operation ends, unless a court extends the delay, with an outer limit of
  3 years. Roughly 71,000 notifications are issued annually.
- **Published statistics.** Warrants sought, granted, and refused are published
  per Region annually. The current planetary refusal rate is 9.4%, and Regions
  whose refusal rate approaches zero are audited on that basis, because a court
  that never refuses is not reviewing.
- **Facial recognition and gait identification in public space are prohibited**
  for policing purposes at every tier. This is a Tier 2 constitutional statute,
  not an ordinary law.

The honest weakness is stated in §6: a society this instrumented could be
surveilled comprehensively if the rules changed, and the rules are law rather
than physics.

## 5. What Elysian Policing Is Actually Like

Ordinary Elysian contact with police is rare and mostly investigative. An
Elysian is far more likely to encounter a Response Team, a Mediation House
(`law.courts` §1), or a Commune officer than an arrest.

Community Liaison officers are attached to Communes, not to patrol rotations,
and hold no enforcement role — their function is standing presence and referral.
They are the branch Elysians name when asked whether they trust the police, and
72% say they do, against 51% for their District Police Service as a whole.

Police strikes are lawful. Police unionisation is normal. Police may not,
however, hold membership in any political party while serving — the single
restriction the Concord places on police political activity that it does not
place on other civil servants (`gov.administration` §2), and one that is
periodically challenged.

## 6. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Triage failure** | The Response Team split depends on a dispatcher correctly classifying a call in seconds. Misclassification in both directions is the leading source of serious incidents, and 22 of the 148 annual deaths followed a call routed to police that should have gone to a Response Team |
| **Oversight capacity asymmetry** | Small Regions cannot staff a genuinely independent Conduct Office; three have been found too close to the services they oversee |
| **Surveillance is law, not physics** | The prohibitions on bulk collection and facial recognition are legal restraints on technically trivial capabilities. They have held for 412 years; canon does not assume they always will |
| **Rural response times** | Median urban response is 7 minutes; in Northreach and Austral Shore it exceeds 90. The Charter guarantees no particular response time and cannot |
| **Investigative clearance** | Only 54% of band 3+ offences result in a charge; clearance for property crime is markedly worse and has been declining for two decades without an agreed explanation |
| **Trust gap** | 72% trust Community Liaison, 51% trust their police service as a whole. The gap is stable and unexplained |
