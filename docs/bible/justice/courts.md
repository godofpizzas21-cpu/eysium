## 1. Five Levels, One of Which Is Not a Court

| Level | Count | Composition | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mediation House** | 47,900 (one per Commune) | Trained mediators, no judges | Voluntary resolution of civil disputes and minor offences |
| **District Court** | 3,400 courthouses across 1,104 District systems | One professional judge + two lay assessors chosen by lot | First instance, criminal and civil |
| **Regional Court of Appeal** | 34 | Three professional judges | Appeal on fact and law |
| **Court of Review** | 1 (45 judges, panels of 5) | Professional judges | Final appeal on questions of law; harmonises law across Regions |
| **Constitutional Court** | 1 (15 justices) | See `gov.institutions` §4 | Constitutional review, competence disputes, emergency review |

The two apex courts are deliberately separate. The Court of Review says what the
law *means*; the Constitutional Court says what the law *may be*. A litigant who
loses on interpretation in the Court of Review may still raise a constitutional
question, but must raise it as a distinct claim before a distinct bench. The
founders separated them because a single supreme court would have concentrated
both powers in fifteen people, which Elysian constitutional instinct treats as a
single point of failure (`cult.foundations` §1).

### Mediation Houses come first

**71% of civil disputes and 34% of minor offences are resolved in a Mediation
House and never reach a court.** Mediation is free, available in any of the 41
registered languages, and normally concluded within 30 days.

It is **voluntary in outcome but compulsory in attendance**: a party may refuse
any settlement, but must attend at least one session before filing most civil
claims. Refusing a reasonable settlement carries no penalty — an explicit
departure from Earth systems that punish it, because the Concord concluded that
cost sanctions on refusal convert voluntary mediation into coerced settlement.

Canon records the corresponding risk honestly in §7: a system that routes
seven-tenths of disputes away from courts also routes them away from published
precedent, and away from the protections a court provides to the weaker party.

## 2. Lay Assessors

Every criminal trial and every significant civil trial at first instance is
heard by **one professional judge and two lay assessors drawn by lot** from the
District's adult residents. Assessors serve for a single case, are paid at full
earnings replacement, and have equal votes with the judge on both verdict and
sentence. A judge outvoted two-to-one must record and publish the fact.

This is the same anti-capture reasoning as the Nominating Assembly
(`gov.institutions` §6): a decision-maker who cannot be selected, promoted, or
retained by anyone has nothing to trade. It also reflects the belief that
findings of fact and judgments of blameworthiness are not expert questions and
should not be made only by people who make them professionally.

Judges are outvoted in 6.2% of cases. That figure is published annually and is
watched closely: Concord legal scholars argue that a rate near zero would prove
the assessors were decorative, and a rate much higher would suggest the
professional judiciary was out of step with the public it serves.

## 3. Judges

**Appointment.** District and Regional judges are appointed by their Region,
which must satisfy three Charter floors: appointment by a body independent of
the regional executive, published reasons for every appointment, and a published
competitive process. Beyond that, methods vary — 21 Regions use judicial
commissions, 9 use commissions with sortition-selected lay majorities, and 4
elect judges from a pre-qualified pool.

Court of Review judges are appointed by the Nominating Assembly from a shortlist
prepared by the Regional judiciaries, and confirmed by the Council of Regions.

**Tenure.** Judges serve to a mandatory retirement age of **95 EY** and may be
removed only for incapacity or serious misconduct, on a finding of the Audit
Service and a two-thirds vote of the appointing body. No judge's salary may be
reduced during their service — a small, old provision that exists because
salary is the quietest lever.

With 112-year lifespans (`hist.demographics` §1) a judge appointed at 40 EY may
serve 55 years. The Concord accepts this at the lower levels and refuses it at
the top: Constitutional Court terms are single, non-renewable, and 18 years
(`gov.institutions` §4).

**Publication.** Every judgment publishes its reasons and every dissent in full.
Judgments are published in the language of the proceedings and translated into
Concordial within 30 days; Court of Review judgments are published in all 41
registered languages.

## 4. The Legal Profession

**Advocates** are admitted regionally and practise planet-wide; there are 3.1
million in the Concord, about 43 per 100,000 residents.

The distinctive feature is that a large share of them are public. The
**Public Legal Service** employs 1.16 million advocates and provides
representation, free at the point of use, to anyone in criminal proceedings and
to anyone below an income threshold in civil proceedings — currently 62% of
households. The Service is organisationally independent of prosecutors, police,
and government, funded on the same constitutional formula model as the
independent offices (`gov.institutions` §6), so that the body defending people
from the state cannot be starved by the state.

**Prosecution** is conducted by regional Prosecution Services, which are
independent of both police and the regional executive. A prosecutor must decline
a charge unless satisfied both that the evidence supports conviction and that
prosecution serves a public purpose, and must publish reasons for declining in
cases involving death or serious injury.

There is no private prosecution and no plea bargaining. Canon is explicit about
why the second is prohibited: a system that trades a sentence for a plea prices
the right to trial, and Charter right 16 does not permit rights to have prices.
The cost is a slower and more expensive criminal process, which the Concord pays.

## 5. Access to Justice

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Median time, filing to first-instance civil judgment | 141 days |
| Median time, charge to criminal verdict | 96 days |
| Civil disputes resolved without court | 71% |
| Households eligible for free civil representation | 62% |
| Criminal defendants represented | 100% |
| Proceedings conducted in the party's own language | 99.4% |
| Cost to a median household of a defended civil claim | 4 days' median income |

**Costs.** The loser pays the winner's costs, but only up to a published scale
capped by the value of the claim, and never at all where the losing party was
below the legal-aid threshold. Uncapped costs were abolished in EY 118 after a
Court of Review judgment held that a costs exposure a litigant could not predict
was a de facto denial of the right to a hearing.

**Language.** Right 13 (`cult.languages` §4) is enforced literally: proceedings
are conducted in the party's language, not translated for them. Where parties
use different languages, the court sits bilingually and the judgment is
authoritative in both. This is expensive and occasionally slow; it is also the
guarantee Elysians name most often when asked what makes their courts fair.

## 6. Constitutional Review in Practice

Any court may find a law incompatible with the Charter, but only the
Constitutional Court may set one aside. A District judge who reaches an
incompatibility must refer the question upward and stay the case. Roughly 340
referrals are made each year; the Constitutional Court accepts about 60.

**Standing is wide.** Any person affected may bring a constitutional claim, and
so may the Office of Future Generations, any Region, the Audit Service, and —
uniquely — any group of 100,000 residents by petition, without needing to show
personal injury. The last route exists because the founders judged that some
constitutional wrongs injure everyone slightly and no one enough to sue.

**Remedies are graduated.** The Court prefers, in order: interpretation that
saves the law, a declaration of incompatibility with a deadline for Parliament
to fix it, suspension, and only last, annulment. This ordering is the humility
clause (`gov.constitution` §7) operating as remedial doctrine — prefer the
outcome that leaves the most room to correct a mistake.

## 7. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Mediation opacity** | 71% of civil disputes resolve without published reasons or precedent. Systematic unfairness inside Mediation Houses would be invisible to the case law, and the Record Office's sampling audits are the only check |
| **Settlement pressure on the weaker party** | Compulsory attendance plus an eager mediator can produce settlements that a court would not have approved. Recognised; partially mitigated by advocate presence rights, not solved |
| **Legal pluralism and forum shopping** | 34 Regions with 34 bodies of civil and criminal law produce genuine forum shopping in commercial and family matters. The Court of Review harmonises slowly and only on appeal |
| **Assessor variance** | Lay assessors vary; outcomes vary with them. The Concord treats this as the price of lay participation and publishes the variance rather than hiding it |
| **Delay at the apex** | The Court of Review's harmonising function is limited by its capacity; a conflict between Regional appellate lines can persist for a decade before resolution |
| **No plea bargaining** | Prohibited on principle, at a real cost in time, money, and the occasional collapse of a case that a negotiated plea would have secured |
