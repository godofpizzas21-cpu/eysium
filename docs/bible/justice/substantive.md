## 1. Whose Law Is It

Criminal and civil law are **regional** competences. The Concord did not take
them, because Article 3 gives it only what is enumerated
(`gov.constitution` §2.1), and thirty-four Regions accordingly have thirty-four
criminal codes and thirty-four civil codes.

Three things hold the system together:

**The Concord Criminal Floor.** A short instrument setting minimum protections
that every regional code must satisfy — the elements below in §2.3. It is a
floor and not a ceiling: a Region may be more protective, never less.

**Planetary offences.** A small enumerated list the Concord does prosecute
directly, because the enumerated powers require it: offences against the
Article 12 systems (including extraction of retained carbon for combustion),
biosecurity and pathogen offences, disarmament violations, corruption in Concord
bodies, offences against the planetary commons, and offences against off-world
installations.

**Model codes.** The Court of Review and the Regional judiciaries jointly
publish model criminal and civil codes, which no Region is required to adopt.
Twenty-six Regions have adopted the model civil code substantially unchanged,
and nineteen the model criminal code. Convergence by attraction rather than by
command is the Concord's standard technique and appears again in Phases 6–11.

## 2. Criminal Law

### 2.1 The harm principle

Every regional code opens from the same premise: **conduct is criminal only if
it harms or endangers an identifiable interest of another person, the public, or
the biosphere.** There are no offences of mere immorality, blasphemy, or
private conduct between consenting adults anywhere in the Concord.

The clearest consequence is that **drug use is not a crime**. Psychoactive
substances are a regulated health matter — licensed supply, quality control,
age limits, and treatment on demand (Phase 9). Supplying outside the regulated
system is an offence; consuming is not, anywhere. This settled during the
Consolidation and is now so uncontroversial that Elysian students find the
pre-founding criminalization genuinely difficult to understand.

The harm principle is applied to the biosphere with full seriousness. Serious
ecological damage is a crime against the public, prosecuted like any other, and
the gravest ecological offences carry the longest sentences in Concord law.

### 2.2 Grading and sentencing

Offences are graded in five bands, and sentencing is governed by three rules
that appear in every regional code because the Criminal Floor requires them.

| Band | Character | Typical disposal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Regulatory and minor | Mediation House, restoration order, fine |
| 2 | Harm without lasting injury | Restorative process, supervision, service |
| 3 | Serious harm | Supervision or custody up to 4 EY |
| 4 | Grave harm | Custody 4–15 EY |
| 5 | Gravest — killing, mass ecological destruction, biosecurity offences, atrocity | Custody 15–30 EY |

**No sentence may be indefinite.** The maximum determinate sentence anywhere in
the Concord is **30 Elysian years**, and there is no life sentence, no sentence
without release date, and no preventive detention of the sane.

**Mandatory review every 5 years.** Any custodial sentence is reviewed by a court
every five years regardless of length, with the burden on the state to justify
continued detention.

**No death penalty** — abolished at the founding and non-derogable
(`gov.constitution` §3).

The 30-year cap is contested. After the Kessander Plague was reconstructed in the
EY 260s and two surviving conspirators were identified, the maximum available
sentence for an offence that killed 340 million people was thirty years, and
Concord public opinion has never entirely accepted that. Two amendment attempts
to create a longer sentence for atrocity have failed, both after adverse
Review Panel opinions (`gov.constitution` §4). Canon records this as a live and
unresolved argument, not a settled virtue.

### 2.3 The Criminal Floor

Every regional code must guarantee:

- Conduct criminal only if defined by law before it occurred; no retroactive
  offences and no retroactive increase in penalty.
- A mental element for every offence carrying custody. Strict liability is
  permitted only for regulatory offences punishable by fine.
- Presumption of innocence, with the burden on the prosecution throughout.
  The only permitted reversals of burden are narrow, listed, and all concern
  retaliation against protected disclosers (`gov.administration` §3).
- Proof beyond reasonable doubt for conviction.
- No double jeopardy, except reopening on newly discovered evidence *in favour
  of the convicted person* — which may be done at any time, without limit.
- Limitation periods for all offences except band 5, which do not expire.
- Proportionality between offence and sentence, reviewable on appeal.

## 3. Rights in Custody and Procedure

The procedural guarantees are the most operationally detailed part of Elysian
law, because the founders' view was that rights are only as real as the moment
of arrest.

- **Counsel from first contact.** A person is entitled to an advocate before any
  questioning, provided free by the Public Legal Service. Questioning conducted
  before counsel arrives is inadmissible for any purpose.
- **Complete recording.** Every custodial interaction is recorded in audio and
  video, held by the Record Office rather than by police, and accessible to the
  detained person and their advocate. A gap in the recording is presumed adverse
  to the state.
- **Production within 48 hours.** No person may be held longer than 48 hours
  without being brought before a judge, in person. There is no exception,
  including under emergency powers — right 5 is non-derogable.
- **Remand limits.** Pre-trial detention requires judicial authorisation renewed
  every 30 days, and trial must begin within 90 days of remand or the person is
  released. 9.4% of the detained population is on remand.
- **Exclusion of evidence.** Evidence obtained in breach of a Charter right is
  inadmissible, and there is no good-faith exception. Derivative evidence is
  excluded unless the state proves an independent source.
- **Reasons and appeal.** Every verdict and sentence publishes reasons; appeal
  lies as of right once on fact and law.

## 4. Wrongful Conviction

Canon treats this as a design problem rather than an embarrassment, which is the
Public Record Act (`hist.timeline` §13) applied to courts.

Every Region maintains a **Review Chamber**: a standing body, independent of the
courts that convicted, which may reopen any conviction at any time on new
evidence or new understanding of old evidence. Applications are free, and the
Chamber may investigate on its own initiative.

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Convictions later overturned as wrongful | 0.7% |
| Median time from conviction to overturn | 6.4 EY |
| Compensation | Automatic, scheduled, without requiring proof of innocence beyond the overturn |
| Mandatory published inquiry into cause | Yes, in every case |

The 0.7% figure is published annually and is understood as a floor on the true
rate, not a measurement of it — a point the Review Chambers make in their own
reports. Elysian legal culture treats the number's existence as more important
than its size: a system that reports no wrongful convictions is assumed to be
failing to look.

## 5. Civil Law

Regional civil codes cover obligations, property, family, and succession. The
model code's structure is followed closely almost everywhere, so the account
below is broadly accurate planet-wide.

**Obligations.** Contract is consensual and largely free, subject to two
distinctive limits: terms are unenforceable if they were not reasonably
comprehensible to the party bound, and any contract term purporting to waive a
Charter right is void. Tort liability is fault-based, with strict liability for
ecological damage and for inherently hazardous activity.

**Property.** Ownership exists and is protected, including the ordinary right to
sell, lend, and bequeath. But land is held subject to the **stewardship
obligation**: an owner may not degrade land's ecological function, and the
obligation runs with the land, binding successors. Land in protected areas
(`planet.biosphere` §6) may be owned but not developed. Elysians do not
experience this as unusual; the concept descends directly from the Alcyon
flow-share, in which water was allocated and never owned (`hist.timeline` §3).

**Family.** Household forms are recognised as they are constituted
(`hist.demographics` §4), including multi-generational and co-residential
households, with registration available to any of them. Dissolution proceeds
through mediation by default. Children's interests are represented separately
and by an advocate they may speak to alone.

**Succession.** With four living generations ordinary, succession law is unusually
developed. Estates commonly pass through several living generations at once, and
a substantial share of large estates is subject to the wealth-transfer rules of
Phase 6 rather than to civil law alone.

## 6. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **The 30-year cap** | Publicly contested, particularly for atrocity; twice sustained by Review Panels against public opinion. Genuinely unresolved |
| **Thirty-four codes** | Real divergence in family and commercial law; forum shopping; harmonisation only through slow appellate convergence |
| **Ecological offences are hard to prosecute** | Diffuse causation, long latency, and corporate structure make band 5 ecological convictions rare relative to the harm done |
| **Wrongful convictions are under-detected** | The 0.7% figure is a floor and known to be one |
| **Procedural cost** | No plea bargaining, full recording, and universal representation make criminal justice slow and expensive by design |
| **Model-code gravity** | Convergence by attraction is voluntary in form; in practice a Region departing from the model code faces friction that looks a little like compulsion |
