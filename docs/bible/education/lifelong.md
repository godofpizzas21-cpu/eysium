## 1. The Entitlement Account

Charter right 10 guarantees education "including lifelong education". It is
delivered through the **Education Entitlement Account**, and this is the single
most consequential difference between Elysian and Earth-style education systems.

Every Elysian receives, on reaching 16 EY, an account containing **12 Elysian
years of fully funded full-time study** beyond upper school. It may be drawn at
any age, in any increment, at any institution, for any subject, in any order, and
it does not expire. Ever.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Entitlement | 12 Elysian years, full-time equivalent |
| Fees | None, at any institution, at any age |
| Maintenance | Paid at the Civic Income plus a study supplement while drawing |
| Expiry | None |
| Median age at first draw | **24 EY** |
| Share of first draws before 20 EY | 31% |
| Enrolled learners over 50 EY | 31% of all post-secondary enrolment |
| Total post-secondary and adult enrolment | 390 million |

**Nobody goes straight from school to university as a matter of course.** The
median Elysian works, travels, or does something else for eight years first, and
returns to study at 24 EY with an idea of what they want it for. Elysian
educationalists regard the Earth pattern — a single continuous run from 5 to 22,
choosing a life at 17 — as a strange artefact of short lifespans, and say so
politely when it comes up.

The entitlement interacts with two other rights. The **fallow entitlement**
(`econ.markets` §3) provides the job-protected time; the **transition right**
(`ind.industry` §3) tops the account up by three years for anyone displaced by
automation, so that displacement never draws down what a person was saving for
something else.

## 2. Institutions

| Type | Count | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Universities | 4,100 | Degree-granting, research-active (Phase 8B) |
| Technical institutes | 6,800 | Applied, credential-granting, workshop-based |
| Research academies | 340 | Postgraduate and specialist; attached to major facilities |
| Commune learning centres | 47,900 | One per Commune; adult, part-time, and community learning |

**Technical institutes are not a lesser track.** They award credentials of equal
legal standing to degrees, their staff are paid on the same scale, and roughly
40% of Elysians hold qualifications from both a university and an institute at
some point in life. The Concord's position, stated in the founding statute and
repeated in every review, is that a civilization whose central project is
maintenance (`cult.foundations` §1) cannot afford a status hierarchy that ranks
knowing above doing.

Canon records honestly in §6 that the position is stated more successfully than
it is achieved.

**Apprenticeship** combines paid employment with institute study, typically 3–4
Elysian years, and is regulated by sectoral agreements
(`econ.markets` §3). Apprentices are employees with full labour rights, not
trainees, and 22% of 16–24 EY Elysians enter one.

## 3. Adult and Community Learning

Every Commune operates a learning centre, and 19% of adult Elysians are enrolled
in something at any given time — most of it part-time, unassessed, and
undertaken for its own sake. The commonest subjects are languages, craft,
music, ecology, and local history.

The centres are also where **retraining** happens: 148 million Elysians are in
substantial retraining at any moment (`econ.markets` §3), and the entitlement
account funds it without any requirement to justify the choice. A person may
retrain into a field with worse prospects than the one they left, and no
administrator may refuse them.

## 4. Libraries

**51,200 libraries** — one in every Commune, plus regional, university, and
specialist collections.

Elysian libraries do something Earth libraries generally do not: they are the
physical access points for the **right of access to record**
(`gov.administration` §5). A librarian is trained to help a resident locate,
request, and appeal for public records, and library staff filed 14% of all access
requests last year on residents' behalf.

This is the Concord's partial answer to the weakness it admits elsewhere — that
it has **solved disclosure and not solved attention** (`gov.administration` §7).
Publishing everything is worthless if nobody can find it, and the library network
is the institution charged with making the record navigable. Canon does not claim
this has worked, only that it is the attempt.

Libraries also hold the physical deposit copy of the permanent archive, in a
distributed arrangement: no single site holds a unique copy of anything, and the
Record Office maintains at least four geographically separated copies of every
permanent record. Redundancy again (`cult.foundations` §1).

## 5. AI Tutors

Every learner in the Concord has access to an AI tutor, free, at every stage.
They are genuinely useful, widely used, and tightly constrained. The governance
regime is Phase 13's; the educational rules belong here.

**An AI tutor may:** explain, question, generate practice, translate between the
41 registered languages, adapt pace, and be available at any hour of a 25.9-hour
day.

**An AI tutor may not:**

- **Assess.** No AI-generated judgement contributes to any portfolio, credential,
  or moderation decision. Assessment is human, and this is a Concord floor.
- **Complete work for a learner.** A tutor asked to produce the work must
  decline and offer to help the learner produce it. Compliance is audited by
  sampling, and vendors failing the audit lose accreditation.
- **Replace a teacher relationship.** Every learner under 16 EY has a named human
  teacher accountable for their education, and tutor availability may not be used
  to justify raising pupil–teacher ratios. Three Regions attempted exactly that
  in the EY 370s and were stopped by the Constitutional Court.
- **Conceal uncertainty.** A tutor must be able to say it does not know, and its
  confidence must be legible to the learner. This requirement descends explicitly
  from the Cassian Incident (`hist.timeline` §8): a system whose correct operation
  is indistinguishable from its failure is not safe to rely on.

**Every interaction is logged**, held by the Record Office rather than by the
provider, and accessible to the learner and — for minors — their guardian. The
logs are not accessible to schools for assessment purposes.

## 6. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Entitlement take-up skews** | Like the fallow entitlement, use rises with income and education. High earners draw a median 9.1 years of the 12; low earners draw 5.4. A universal right is used most by those already advantaged |
| **Status hierarchy persists** | Technical institutes are legally equal and socially not. Institute graduates are under-represented in the Executive Board, the Court of Review, and senior administration, and the gap has narrowed only slightly in two centuries |
| **Late entry has costs** | Entering study at 24 EY works well for most subjects and poorly for a few — mathematics, music performance, and some languages show measurably better outcomes with early intensive study, and the Concord has never resolved how to offer that without reintroducing selection |
| **Institution concentration** | Prestige clusters in about forty institutions, and their graduates are over-represented everywhere. No mechanism has reduced this |
| **AI tutor dependence** | Sampling finds 11% of upper-school learners using tutors in ways that would fail the completion rule if the tutor were more compliant; enforcement is against vendors, not learners, and the boundary is genuinely blurry |
| **Library attention gap** | 14% of access requests are library-assisted, which is a real contribution and nowhere near enough for the problem it addresses |
