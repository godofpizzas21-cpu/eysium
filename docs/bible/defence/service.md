## 1. What It Is For

The Concord has no external enemy. There is one polity on Elysium and no
contacted civilization beyond it (`res.sciences` §2). This single fact determines
everything about Elysian defence, and Elysians state it plainly: **the Concord
maintains a service, not an army.**

The **Concord Service** exists to do six things:

| Purpose | Branch | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Disaster response, rescue, and restoration | **Response Corps** | 4,100,000 |
| Space traffic, debris, and orbital rescue | Orbital Guard | 210,000 |
| Defence of networks and public infrastructure | Cyber Defence Directorate | 124,000 |
| Detection and deflection of impacting bodies | Planetary Defence Directorate | 86,000 |
| Enforcement of the Abolition | Verification Inspectorate | 41,000 |
| Armed contingency | **Standing Force** | 340,000 |

**Total: 4.9 million** — smaller than the District Police Services at 6.38
million (`law.policing` §1), and 84% of it is the Response Corps. Defence
spending is 1.1% of GCP, of which the armed component is 0.09%.

The proportions are the argument. A civilization that spends eleven times more on
rescuing people from storms than on the capacity to fight is making a claim about
itself, and Elysians make it deliberately.

## 2. Doctrine

Elysian defence doctrine is three words, and they are the same three that govern
its answer to earthquakes, epidemics, and grid failure:

> **Early detection. Absorbed impact. Rapid restoration.**

The Concord does not attempt to prevent the events it faces. Cyclones, impacts,
pathogens, and grid cascades will occur. Doctrine is therefore about **shortening
the interval between a thing happening and the system noticing**, and about
building systems that bend rather than break.

This is why the 14-day Commune islanding rule (`energy.grid` §1), the two-year
strategic reserves (`ind.industry` §6), the 14-month food holding
(`agri.security` §2), and the 4% District emergency housing capacity
(`city.housing` §5) all belong to defence policy as much as to their own domains.
The Concord's defence budget is small because most of its defensive spending is
recorded under other headings.

## 3. Military Ethics

The Service is bound by rules that are stricter than the Charter requires,
because the founders wrote them while the Long Emergency was still recent.

- **The oath is to the Charter**, not to the Concord, the Executive Board, or any
  person. Its operative clause is a duty to refuse.
- **The duty to refuse a manifestly unlawful order** is absolute, individual, and
  carries no penalty for good-faith refusal. It is the most-taught provision in
  Service formation, for a reason every Elysian knows: the Cassian Incident was
  averted by one officer exercising exactly this duty over ninety seconds
  (`hist.timeline` §8).
- **No autonomous lethal systems.** Target selection and the decision to use
  force require an identified human at every escalation point. Prohibited
  absolutely under `res.system` §3 and enforced as a planetary offence.
- **No automated escalation.** No system may move a posture upward without human
  action. Systems may de-escalate automatically; they may never escalate.
- **No secret forces and no secret budget.** Every unit, its strength, and its
  funding are published. Operational details may be withheld for a maximum of
  three years and then publish automatically.
- **Service members retain full Charter rights**, including expression,
  association, and — with narrow operational exceptions — the right to organise.
- **No conscription.** Service is voluntary and the Charter forbids compulsion.

## 4. Command

The Service is commanded by the **Resilience and Defence** portfolio holder,
who is one of nine on a collegial Executive Board and cannot act alone
(`gov.institutions` §3). Three constraints define command:

- **Deployment of the Standing Force within a Region requires that Region's
  request, or an order of the Constitutional Court.** There is no other route.
  The provision has never been used against a Region's wishes, including during
  the withdrawal crises of EY 22 and EY 31, which were resolved by negotiation.
- **Any use of force requires Board decision**, not portfolio decision, and is
  reported to the Assembly within 24 civil hours.
- **Emergency powers do not enlarge command.** The 30-day lapse, 180-day cap, and
  automatic judicial review apply identically (`gov.constitution` §6), and
  emergency powers may not be used to postpone an election or extend a term.

## 5. The Abolition Regime

The **Abolition** — verified dismantling of all strategic weapons — completed in
EY 34 (`hist.timeline` §10). It is Tier 1 entrenched, cannot be withdrawn from
even by a departing Region (`gov.constitution` §5), and is enforced by the
**Verification Inspectorate**.

| Instrument | Practice |
|---|---|
| Material accounting | Continuous accounting of fissile material, tritium inventories, and neutron sources across all 6,200 fusion plants |
| Right of access | The Inspectorate may enter any facility, in any Region, without notice, without stating cause |
| Pathogen oversight | Joint with the Biosecurity portfolio at all 41 Concord containment facilities and every licensed synthetic-biology site |
| Orbital accounting | Every object above 10 cm catalogued and attributed to an operator |
| Publication | All findings publish, including inconclusive ones and the Inspectorate's own access refusals |
| Enforcement | Breach is a planetary offence prosecuted directly by the Concord |

**The dual-use problem is the regime's permanent difficulty and canon states it
openly.** A civilization running 6,200 fusion plants necessarily produces
tritium, neutron sources, and the industrial competence to weaponise them. The
Abolition does not — cannot — remove the capability; it removes the *stockpile*
and makes reconstitution slow, visible, and criminal. The Inspectorate's own
annual assessment states that a determined Region could reconstitute a strategic
capability in roughly four years and could not do so unnoticed for more than
eight months.

Elysians find this reassuring rather than alarming, and the reasoning is
characteristic: the Abolition was never a guarantee that nobody *could*. It is an
arrangement in which anyone who tried would be seen doing it, by an inspectorate
they cannot bar, while everyone else still had years to respond.

**Seventeen inspection findings** have triggered formal proceedings since EY 34.
All seventeen concerned accounting failures rather than concealment, and all
seventeen were resolved without prosecution — a record the Inspectorate itself
describes in its reports as *"either the system working or the system not
looking hard enough, and we cannot fully distinguish these."*

## 6. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Dual-use is unsolvable** | Fusion infrastructure is reconstitution infrastructure; the regime buys time and visibility, not impossibility |
| **The Inspectorate's own uncertainty** | Seventeen findings, none of concealment, and the Inspectorate publicly declines to claim this proves absence |
| **No contested experience** | The Standing Force has not fought since EY 1. Its doctrine, equipment, and command are untested against an adversary, and canon does not pretend simulation substitutes |
| **A service optimised for disaster** | 84% of the Service is a rescue organisation. If the Concord ever needed an army it would need years, and the arrangement assumes it would have them |
| **Deployment veto cuts both ways** | A Region in genuine constitutional breakdown could refuse the deployment that would help it, and only the Court could override — slowly |
| **Publication versus operations** | Full publication of units, strengths, and budgets is a real operational constraint; the Service argues for exceptions roughly once a decade and has never won one |
