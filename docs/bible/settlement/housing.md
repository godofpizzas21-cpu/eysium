## 1. The Right and What It Means

Charter right 8 guarantees "housing adequate to health and dignity". It is a
**provision right** — subject to resource limits and enforceable as a floor
(`gov.constitution` §3) — and it is the most litigated of the provision rights,
because "adequate" is a word courts have had to fill in.

The settled interpretation, established by the Court of Review across a century
of cases, is that adequate housing must be: secure in tenure, affordable against
income, physically fit and warm, accessible to the occupant's needs, within reach
of the twenty-minute service set (`city.urbanism` §3), and **not conditional on
behaviour**. That last is the one Elysians argue about least and other
civilizations would find most surprising: a District may not make housing
contingent on employment, sobriety, treatment compliance, or good conduct.

Duty falls on the **District** (`gov.regions` §4). A person without adequate
housing may apply to their District and, if refused, to a court. Districts
losing such cases are named in the Record Office's quarterly return.

## 2. Tenure

| Tenure | Share of households |
|---|---|
| Owner-occupied | 44% |
| **Cooperative and mutual housing** | 24% |
| Public and social rented | 21% |
| Private rented | 11% |

2.13 billion households, mean size 3.4.

**Cooperative housing is the distinctive tier.** A housing cooperative owns its
buildings collectively; members hold a right of occupancy rather than a tradeable
asset, pay cost rent rather than market rent, and govern the cooperative one
member one vote. Members may leave and recover their contribution indexed to
construction costs, not to land values — so the housing cannot be sold out from
under the next generation, and nobody profits from the shortage.

Cooperatives grew from 4% to 24% of households over three centuries without ever
being mandated, by the same "convergence by attraction" mechanism as worker
cooperatives (`econ.markets` §1): public land leases favour them, finance is
available on cost-rent terms, and conversion carries tax relief.

**Private renting is small and heavily regulated.** Indefinite tenancies by
default, rent increases capped to a published index, eviction only for specified
cause through a court, and a licensing regime for landlords. The sector has
shrunk steadily and Concord housing policy neither encourages nor prohibits it.

## 3. Cost

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Median housing cost, share of median household income | **14.1%** |
| Cooperative cost rent, share of income | 11.2% |
| Private rent, share of income | 19.4% |
| Households spending over 30% on housing | 3.9% |

Housing is cheap on Elysium relative to any Earth benchmark, and the reasons are
structural rather than clever: land value tax removes most speculative return,
leasehold separates land from building, cooperatives price at cost, off-site
manufacture keeps construction costs falling, and — the largest factor — supply
is permitted. A District that fails to meet housing need faces a legal duty it
can be sued over, which turns out to be a more effective driver of construction
than any incentive.

## 4. Homelessness

**2.2 million Elysians — 0.03% of the population — experienced homelessness at
some point in the last year.** The figure is published annually and canon states
it rather than claiming the problem solved.

Of those, 84% were rehoused within 30 days, 11% within a season, and roughly
94,000 people experienced homelessness lasting more than a year. That residual is
overwhelmingly associated with severe mental illness, dependence, and — in a
minority of cases — a settled refusal of the housing offered.

The Concord's response is **housing first, unconditionally**: an offer is made
without preconditions, is not withdrawn on refusal, and is repeated. Support is
offered alongside and may be declined without affecting the housing. Where a
person declines housing entirely, no one may compel them, because Charter right 3
protects freedom of residence and there is no vagrancy offence anywhere in the
Concord (`law.substantive` §2.1).

Canon's honest position: the Concord has made homelessness rare, brief, and
non-punitive, and has not eliminated it. The residual is a problem of illness and
autonomy rather than of housing supply, and the housing system cannot solve it
alone.

## 5. Special Provision

- **On release from custody**, housing is guaranteed and arranged before release,
  not applied for after (`law.corrections` §4).
- **On managed retreat**, replacement is at full value with the Commune
  relocated together where residents choose (`env.climate` §4).
- **After disaster**, emergency housing capacity is held at District level for
  4% of District population, exercised annually (Phase 12).
- **For frailty**, adaptation is funded on assessment rather than on means, and
  is delivered within 45 days in 88% of cases.

## 6. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Housing wealth compounds** | 44% owner-occupation plus 112-year lifespans means long-tenured households in high-demand Districts accumulate substantial appreciated wealth despite land value tax; a principal driver of the flat wealth Gini |
| **Homelessness is not zero** | 94,000 people homeless for over a year; the residual is a health and autonomy problem the housing system cannot reach |
| **District duty is unevenly enforced** | The right is only as strong as a person's willingness to litigate; the Public Legal Service covers it, and take-up among the people most affected is lowest |
| **Cooperative entry is rationed** | Cooperatives are the cheapest tenure and have waiting lists averaging 2.4 Elysian years; entry favours those with existing connections to a cooperative |
| **Adaptation delays** | 88% within 45 days means 12% wait longer, and the delays concentrate in Regions with the oldest populations and thinnest administrations |
| **Rural housing quality** | Housing that predates the 150-year standard is concentrated in small Aurorian and Veydran settlements, where replacement is uneconomic and subsidy has not closed the gap |
