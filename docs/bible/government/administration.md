## 1. The Nine Portfolios

Each member of the Executive Board holds one portfolio and chairs the
corresponding Concord department. No member may direct another's portfolio
(`gov.institutions` §3).

| Portfolio | Scope |
|---|---|
| **Atmosphere and Ocean** | The Article 12 climate and ocean systems |
| **Commons and Orbit** | Deep ocean, polar ice, orbital space, off-world territory |
| **Resilience and Defence** | Defence, disarmament verification, disaster response |
| **Biosecurity and Health Security** | Epidemic response, pathogen governance |
| **Networks** | Interregional transport, grid, communications backbone |
| **Treasury and Materials** | Currency, monetary policy, Constrained List, strategic reserves |
| **Rights and Legal Affairs** | Rights enforcement, legislative drafting, Court liaison |
| **External Relations** | Diplomacy, treaties, first-contact preparedness |
| **Public Administration** | The civil service, records, delivery standards |

Nine portfolios for a planet of 7.25 billion is a startlingly small executive,
and it is only possible because the Concord tier does very little directly
(`gov.regions` §1). The Concord's departments overwhelmingly set standards,
operate planetary infrastructure, and coordinate; they do not deliver services.

## 2. The Civil Service

**Size:** 579 million public servants across all tiers — 1.9% at the Concord
tier, 21% regional, 63% district, 14% commune. This is 16.0% of the labour force
(`econ.industry`), and it is large because Districts deliver healthcare,
education, transit, and utilities directly.

### Entry and tenure

- **Open competitive examination**, administered by the Public Service
  Commission — a body within the Public Administration portfolio but with
  statutorily protected examination independence. Examinations are offered in
  all 41 registered languages and no language proficiency may be required
  (`cult.languages` §4).
- **No political appointments below the Board.** The Executive Board is
  political; everything beneath it is career service. An incoming Board inherits
  its officials and cannot dismiss them.
- **Tenure is protected** against dismissal except for cause, established
  through a published process with a right of appeal.
- **Political neutrality** is required in the exercise of office and *not*
  required outside it: Elysian civil servants may join parties, campaign, and
  stand for office, provided they take leave to do so and recuse from anything
  they touched. Canon notes this is more permissive than most Earth systems and
  that the Concord considers the alternative — a service of people with no
  political opinions — a fiction.

### The rotation rule

Officials in **exposed posts** — procurement, licensing, inspection, planning
consent, and audit — rotate every 5 Elysian years and may not return to the same
post for 15. This is the single most disliked personnel rule in Concord
government, because it removes experienced people from roles they have mastered.
It exists because the Corran network took eleven years to build and could not
have been built in five.

### The reasons discipline

Because Charter right 15 requires reasons for every exercise of public power,
Elysian administration is *written* to an unusual degree. Every decision affecting
a person generates a published reasoned record. The workload is enormous and
openly acknowledged as a cost; roughly 9% of civil service time goes to it. The
Concord's position, restated in every review since EY 96, is that undocumented
discretion is where capture lives.

## 3. Integrity Systems

The Corran Scandal (EY 187) is the origin of most of what follows. A regional
procurement network captured its own auditors for eleven years and was exposed
not by an investigator but by a data-publication requirement its architects had
opposed as pointless. Every mechanism below reflects that lesson: **publication
catches what inspection misses.**

**Open contracting by default.** Every public contract at every tier — tender,
award, variation, payment, and completion assessment — is published in a
standard machine-readable format within 30 days. There is no threshold below
which contracts are exempt. Commercial confidentiality may redact specific
prices for a maximum of 3 years, and the redaction itself is published.

**Asset and interest disclosure.** All elected officials, Board members,
justices, independent office heads, and civil servants in exposed posts publish
assets, income, and interests annually, and again on leaving office and at
2 and 5 years afterwards. The post-departure filings exist because the payoff
usually arrives later than the favour.

**The conflict registry.** Any official must register a conflict before acting,
not after. Acting on an unregistered conflict is an offence in itself,
independent of whether the underlying decision was improper — a deliberate
choice to make the procedural breach prosecutable, because proving corrupt
intent is hard and proving a missing filing is not.

**Two-key authorisation.** No single official may authorise a payment,
licence, or consent above a modest threshold. The second key must come from a
different reporting line, and the pairing is randomised.

**Random deep audit.** Beyond risk-based auditing, the Audit Service selects
2% of all public bodies annually **by lot** for a full unannounced audit. The
lottery is public and its draw is verifiable. Its purpose is not detection
efficiency but uncertainty: a body that cannot predict whether it will be
audited behaves as though it will be.

**Protected disclosure.** Any person may report suspected wrongdoing to the
Audit Service, which must acknowledge within 5 days and report on outcome.
Retaliation against a discloser is a criminal offence with a reversed burden of
proof: adverse treatment within 3 years of a disclosure is presumed retaliatory
unless the employer proves otherwise. Anonymous disclosure is accepted and
protected equally.

**Cooling-off.** 10 years for independent office heads and senior regulators;
5 years for exposed-post officials; 5 years for Board members. During cooling-off
the official receives a partial continuing salary, on the principle that an
unpaid restriction is a restriction only for people who can afford it.

## 4. Does It Work?

Canon answers this honestly, because Article 15 would require it anyway.

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Public procurement value flagged by audit annually | 0.7% |
| Value recovered after adjudication | 0.4% |
| Officials prosecuted for corruption offences per year | 1,340 |
| Convictions | 61% of prosecutions |
| Public bodies receiving an adverse audit opinion annually | 2.1% |
| Residents reporting having been asked for a bribe (annual survey) | 0.3% |
| Residents believing corruption is common in their District | 11% |
| Residents believing corruption is common at the Concord tier | 19% |

Two things stand out and both are recorded deliberately.

**Corruption has not been eliminated.** It is small, prosecuted, and mostly
petty — and the Concord's own Audit Service states in every annual report that
the residual rate is not zero and will not become zero. The design goal was
never elimination; it was making capture expensive, slow, and likely to surface.

**Perception exceeds reality, and rises with distance.** Elysians believe there
is more corruption at the planetary tier than in their own District, though
measured rates run the other way. The Audit Service attributes this partly to
salience — planetary scandals are planetary news — and partly to the Concord
tier's remoteness. Nobody has fixed it, and canon does not pretend it is merely
an information problem.

## 5. Transparency in Practice

**Publication by default.** Every record of every public body is published
unless an exception applies. Exceptions are exhaustively listed in the Charter's
implementing statute: active security operations, personal data, live commercial
negotiation, and legal privilege. Each carries a maximum duration; each expires
automatically; each expiry is itself logged.

**Deliberative material is open after 3 years.** There is no permanent cabinet
secrecy on Elysium. Executive Board minutes, internal advice, and rejected
options publish automatically at 3 years, and the knowledge that they will
publish is understood to be the main disciplinary effect.

**The refusal ledger.** Every refusal of an access request is published — the
request, the exemption claimed, and the decision-maker. The Record Office
adjudicates appeals and publishes its overturn rate quarterly: currently **34%**
of appealed refusals are overturned. Bodies with persistently high overturn
rates are named, which works considerably better than the fines that preceded it.

**The failure requirement.** Under the Public Record Act, every public body
publishes an annual account of its own failures — what it got wrong, what it
cost, and what changed as a result. Bodies that publish no failures are audited
on that basis, since the Audit Service treats a flawless self-report as evidence
of either inattention or concealment.

## 6. Digital Administration

Public administration runs on **public infrastructure**, not vendor platforms:
identity, payments, records, and case management are all publicly built and
publicly operated, with source published. The reasoning is stated in statute —
a state that cannot read its own systems cannot be audited, and a state that
cannot leave a supplier is governed by it.

Automated decision-making in administration is governed by the AI regime of
Phase 13. Three constraints belong here because they are administrative law
rather than AI policy: no decision affecting a person's rights may be made
without an identified human official accountable for it; the reasons requirement
applies identically to automated decisions; and any person may demand a
non-automated reconsideration.

## 7. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Documentation burden** | 9% of civil service time goes to producing reasons; the cost is real and permanently contested |
| **Rotation and expertise** | The 5-year rotation removes mastery from exactly the posts where mastery is most useful |
| **Perception gap** | Trust in the planetary tier is lower than its measured performance; unexplained and unfixed |
| **Capacity asymmetry** | Small Regions cannot staff the integrity apparatus the Charter assumes |
| **Publication without readership** | Everything is published; comparatively little is read. Transparency depends on intermediaries — journalists, researchers, and auditors — whose own capacity is not constitutionally guaranteed |
| **Residual corruption** | Small, persistent, and openly reported as ineliminable |

The fifth is the one Concord scholars consider most serious, and canon states it
plainly: **the Concord has solved disclosure and not solved attention.** A right
of access to record is worth what someone's willingness to read the record is
worth, and no constitution can compel that.
