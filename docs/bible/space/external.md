## 1. The Orbital Territory Problem

The Orbital Territory is the Concord's 34th Region. It holds 28 million people,
four Council seats like every other Region, and a constitutional position that
canon has flagged as unresolved since Phase 4B and now sets out fully.

**Four things about it break assumptions the Charter makes.**

**It has no territory.** Article 3 distributes powers to Regions over land. The
Orbital Territory's Districts are organised by *installation* — the Low Ring, the
Kalyra settlements, Vesper, the Belt stations — which move, are built, and are
decommissioned. A District can cease to exist because a habitat reached end of
life.

**Its representation is extreme.** Four Council seats for 28 million people is
one seat per 7 million; **Kessandra**, the most populous Region, receives one per
88.75 million — a ratio of nearly thirteen to one. The Council's equal
representation was designed to protect the small from the large
(`gov.institutions` §2.2), and the Orbital Territory is the case where the
principle produces a number nobody would have chosen deliberately.

**Its residents are mostly temporary.** 61% of the Orbital Territory's population
is on rotation and holds residence in a terrestrial Region as well. They vote
where they are registered, and the Territory's settled population — people who
live there, raise children there, and intend to stay — is roughly 9 million.

**The withdrawal procedure does not work for it.** The Fourth Amendment
(`gov.constitution` §5) assumes a Region with borders, a population that stays
put, and shared obligations that can be settled and separated. None of these
apply. Whether the Orbital Territory could lawfully withdraw is genuinely
unknown, and the Constitutional Court has twice declined to answer the question
in the abstract.

**Three reforms are perennially proposed and none has passed:** converting the
Territory to a non-Region status with representation but no Council seats;
splitting it into several Regions by location; and leaving it exactly as it is on
the ground that no arrangement will fit and this one at least works. The third
position is winning by default, which is how canon describes it.

## 2. External Relations

The Concord operates an **External Relations** portfolio — one of nine on the
Executive Board (`gov.administration` §1) — for a job it has never had to do.

There is no other polity on Elysium. There is no contacted civilization
elsewhere. The portfolio's actual work is:

- **First-contact preparedness** (§3), which is a policy exercise rather than a
  response to anything.
- **Custody of the compact regime**: the 340 interregional compacts
  (`gov.regions` §6), which are the nearest thing the Concord has to treaties and
  are made between Regions rather than with anyone outside.
- **The Thalassar lineage**: maintaining the fisheries framework
  (`agri.production` §4) that descends from the first binding planetary treaty and
  is the Concord's only true inherited international instrument.
- **Doctrine of contact**, held ready.

Elysians find the portfolio slightly comic and defend it seriously. Its standing
justification is that a civilization which has never needed diplomacy is exactly
the civilization that would be worst at it if it suddenly did, and that
maintaining the capacity is cheap.

## 3. First Contact

The Concord has surveyed for technological signatures since EY 96 and found
nothing: **41 candidates, 41 resolved** (`res.sciences` §2). The silence is a
scientific problem it cannot address and a policy problem it prepares for anyway.

### The Reception Protocol

The protocol answers a question the Concord's own constitution makes hard:
**who speaks for Elysium?**

There is no head of state, no capital city, and no individual with authority to
respond. The Convenor's own standard formula is *"I speak for a decision, not for
a people"* (`gov.institutions` §3). The Concord's answer is procedural rather
than personal:

1. **Verification first.** No response of any kind until the Kalyra Far-Side
   Array and at least two independent installations confirm. The 41 resolved
   candidates are why.
2. **Mandatory disclosure.** Confirmation publishes immediately and planet-wide.
   No verification period may be conducted in secret; the Record Office holds the
   raw data from the moment of detection.
3. **No individual may respond.** Not the Convenor, not the Board, not a Region,
   not a private operator. Unauthorised transmission is a planetary offence.
4. **The Assembly convenes.** Any reply requires a resolution of the Assembly
   with Council concurrence, after a Review Panel of 300 citizens chosen by lot
   has sat and published (`gov.constitution` §4).
5. **What is said publishes before it is sent.**

The protocol is slow by construction — realistically **months** from confirmation
to any reply — and its critics say so loudly. Its defenders answer that a
civilization which took four years to write its own constitution is not going to
improvise its first sentence to another species, and that the alternative is
someone deciding alone.

### Transmission

**The Concord does not deliberately broadcast.** It listens, it leaks the ordinary
emissions of an industrial civilization, and it has declined since EY 231 to
transmit intentional messages toward candidate systems.

The decision is restraint applied to a question nobody can resolve
(`cult.foundations` §1): the Concord cannot estimate the risks of announcing
itself, cannot estimate the benefits, and has concluded that an irreversible act
under total uncertainty is exactly the kind of act the humility clause
disfavours (`gov.constitution` §7).

**It is genuinely contested.** The position has been challenged in the Assembly
eleven times, most recently in EY 401, and the margin has narrowed. Its opponents
argue that a civilization which believes in candour above almost everything has
chosen silence, and that the inconsistency should trouble it. Canon records the
argument as live and the policy as holding.

## 4. If Contact Occurred

Canon states what is prepared and does not pretend to know what would happen.

- The Concord has **no framework for treating with a non-Elysian polity**, and
  its constitutional machinery assumes participants who are all Elysian.
- Charter rights are written for Elysians. **Whether they would extend to a
  non-Elysian intelligence is unanswered**, and connects directly to the
  unresolved question of artificial moral status (`ai.governance` §6) — the same
  Advisory Panel handles both, and its minority position applies with equal
  force: the criteria were written by beings with an interest in the answer.
- The Concord has **no external migration policy**, because it has never had an
  external population (`hist.demographics` §5).
- The Abolition would raise an immediate question the Concord has deliberately
  not answered in advance: whether a planetary disarmament regime survives the
  arrival of someone who was not party to it.

The External Relations portfolio publishes a contact-readiness assessment every
four years. Its conclusion has been the same for eleven consecutive editions:
*the Concord is well prepared to detect, adequately prepared to verify, poorly
prepared to decide, and not prepared at all to be wrong.*

## 5. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **The Orbital Territory fits nothing** | No territory, extreme representation, a mostly temporary population, and a withdrawal procedure that cannot be applied. Three reforms proposed, none passed, and the status quo wins by default |
| **Nine million settled people, four Council seats** | Whichever number is used, the representation is indefensible on one measure or the other |
| **The Reception Protocol is slow** | Months from confirmation to reply, defended on principle and never tested |
| **Silence contradicts candour** | The transmission policy sits awkwardly beside the Concord's central virtue, and its opponents say so at every review |
| **Rights are species-specific** | Charter rights are written for Elysians, and nobody knows what that means for anyone else |
| **The Abolition after contact** | Deliberately unanswered in advance; canon does not resolve it either |
