## 1. Four Tiers of Protection

44.0% of land (79.7 M km²) and 38.0% of ocean (133.6 M km²) hold permanent
protected status (`planet.biosphere` §6). Protection is not uniform.

| Tier | Land | Ocean | Permitted activity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inviolable** (Article 12) | 3.4 M km² | 84.0 M km² | Nothing extractive. Research under licence only. Cannot be reduced by any means short of a Tier 1 amendment |
| **Strict reserve** | 21.1 M km² | 24.6 M km² | Non-extractive access, monitoring, restoration |
| **Managed conservation** | 38.9 M km² | 21.4 M km² | Traditional use, low-impact harvest, tourism under quota |
| **Connectivity corridor** | 16.3 M km² | 3.6 M km² | Working landscapes managed for passage; farming and forestry permitted under conditions |

The inviolable tier comprises the abyssal plain, the Veydran Ice Cap, the
Verdanne core, and the Myriad hydrothermal fields. Between them they are the
largest and the oldest intact things the Concord has, and the Charter treats them
as unavailable rather than merely valuable.

**The ratchet.** Protected boundaries may be *enlarged* by ordinary regional or
Concord legislation. They may be *reduced* only by Tier 2 amendment, following a
published adverse finding by the Ecological Commission, and never for the
inviolable tier. Protection is therefore easy to add and hard to remove — an
asymmetry the founders chose knowing it would occasionally protect the wrong
thing forever.

**Connectivity is the design principle.** 71% of protected land sits in
connected networks rather than isolated blocks, because a reserve that a species
cannot leave is a large enclosure rather than a habitat. The corridor tier is the
Concord's main instrument here, and it is deliberately a *working* landscape:
farms, managed forest, and even transport corridors qualify if they meet passage
standards. Elysian conservation does not equate protection with absence of
people.

## 2. Restoration

**6.4 M km² — 3.5% of all land — is under active restoration** at any time
(`planet.biosphere` §6), the largest sustained land-use programme in Elysian
history.

| Programme | Extent | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Forest and woodland restoration | 2.9 M km² | Mostly former marginal cultivation returning to temperate broadleaf and taiga |
| Wetland and floodplain reconnection | 1.4 M km² | Alcyon basin and Serapht margins; flood control and carbon in one act |
| Grassland and soil restoration | 1.1 M km² | Meridian Plain margins; soil is accumulating planet-wide |
| Coastal and reef | 0.6 M km² | Living shorelines, kelp-analogue, Stonebloom |
| Post-industrial and mine sites | 0.4 M km² | Funded by restoration bonds (`ind.industry` §4) |

Restoration is slow and Elysians say so. A restored temperate forest reaches
approximate functional equivalence in 80–140 Elysian years and full structural
equivalence in several centuries. The Concord's zero pure time preference
(`econ.money` §5) is what makes these projects fundable at all — under
conventional discounting almost none of them would clear an appraisal.

## 3. Biodiversity

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Described multicellular species | 2.41 million |
| Estimated true total | 4.8 million |
| Complete reference genomes | 1.06 million |
| Industrial-era extinctions attributable to Elysians | 4,180 |
| Species under active recovery | 11,400 |
| Functionally extinct in the wild, preserved in living collections | 260 |
| Species returned to the wild from living collections | 14 |

**Extinction debt is the uncomfortable number.** The Commission's own estimate is
that between 9,000 and 14,000 further species are committed to extinction by
habitat loss already incurred, and will disappear over the coming three to eight
centuries regardless of present policy. Recovery programmes address perhaps a
tenth of them. This figure is published annually, is not disputed, and is the
strongest counter-argument to any account of the Concord as ecologically
successful.

### De-extinction

Genuine de-extinction — reconstructing a species from preserved genetic material
rather than breeding from living collections — has been attempted three times and
succeeded partially once, producing a population of an Aurorian browsing species
that is genetically incomplete and ecologically unproven.

The Concord's licensing regime is restrictive and the reason is not technical.
The dominant objection, made most forcefully by the Office of Future
Generations, is **moral hazard**: a civilization that believes extinction is
reversible will take risks that a civilization that believes it is permanent will
not. The counter-argument — that refusing an available repair to preserve a
useful fear is itself a moral position — has never been answered to anyone's
satisfaction. Canon records the argument and not a resolution.

## 4. Pollution

With no fossil combustion anywhere (`planet.resources` §7), Elysian air is clean
by any historical standard: particulate and nitrogen-oxide concentrations sit at
roughly 4% of Integration-era levels, and air pollution is no longer a
significant cause of death (Phase 9).

What remains is harder.

**The chemical regime.** No substance may enter commerce without a demonstrated
degradation pathway and a published safety dossier. The burden is on the
registrant, not on the regulator: a substance is prohibited until shown
acceptable, rather than permitted until shown harmful. Roughly 41,000 substances
are registered; about 900 applications are refused annually.

**Legacy persistence is the failure the regime cannot fix.** Persistent
compounds manufactured before the founding — fluorinated surfactants, certain
brominated compounds, and a family of industrial solvents — are still cycling
through soil, water, and bodies. Their concentrations are declining, slowly:
current burdens are 34% of the Integration peak and falling by roughly 0.9% a
year. Complete clearance is projected for the EY 600s. Nothing available can
accelerate it materially.

**Microplastics**, similarly, are a legacy problem. Ocean burden stands at 41% of
peak and is falling as inputs approach zero and material is buried or degraded,
but sediment concentrations will remain elevated for millennia.

**Nutrient runoff** from agriculture is the largest current pollution flow and
the only one that is a live policy fight rather than a legacy cleanup. The
phosphorus recovery loop (`planet.resources` §6) captures 92%; the remaining 8%
still produces seasonal coastal hypoxia in four Regions (Phase 11).

**Light and noise** are regulated more seriously than Earth practice would
suggest. Dark-sky standards apply to 61% of land, driven by a combination of
ecological evidence, astronomy (Phase 8), and the cultural place of the aurora,
which reaches mid-latitudes on Elysium (`planet.physical` §3). Elysians regard a
sky you cannot see the aurora from as a defect in a city.

## 5. Enforcement

The Ecological Commission measures and publishes; it does not enforce. Enforcement
runs through ordinary law: serious ecological damage is a crime against the public
under the harm principle, and the gravest ecological offences sit in band 5
alongside killing (`law.substantive` §2.1–2.2).

Canon repeats the weakness recorded in Phase 5A because it belongs here too:
**ecological offences are hard to prosecute.** Causation is diffuse, latency is
long, and responsibility is distributed through corporate structures. Band 5
ecological convictions run at roughly 40 per year planet-wide, which nobody
believes reflects the harm actually done. The main practical deterrent is not
prosecution but the material passport regime and open contracting
(`ind.industry` §1), which make ecological damage visible in records before it
becomes visible in ecosystems.

## 6. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Extinction debt** | 9,000–14,000 species committed to extinction by damage already done; recovery reaches perhaps a tenth. Published annually and undisputed |
| **Legacy persistence** | Pre-founding compounds at 34% of peak, clearing around EY 600, with no available acceleration |
| **Prosecution gap** | ~40 band 5 ecological convictions a year against harm nobody believes is that small |
| **Paper protection** | Protected status is planetary; enforcement is regional, and the Audit Service has found inadequate monitoring in six Regions with small administrations |
| **Restoration lag** | Functional equivalence takes 80–140 years, so present policy is judged on results nobody alive at its adoption will see |
| **The ratchet cuts both ways** | Boundaries drawn at the founding are nearly impossible to correct; three protected areas are now known to be poorly sited and remain protected anyway |
| **De-extinction unresolved** | Licensing is restrictive on a moral-hazard argument that has never been answered, only asserted more loudly by both sides |
| **Nutrient runoff** | 8% loop leakage still produces seasonal hypoxia in four Regions |
