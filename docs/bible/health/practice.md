## 1. Geriatric Medicine Is the Core Specialty

68% of Elysian deaths are age-associated multi-system decline
(`hist.demographics` §6). 710 million Elysians are over 100 EY. Geriatric
medicine is therefore not a subspecialty on Elysium — it is the centre of the
profession, the largest training pathway, and the discipline that shapes how
everything else is practised.

The organising concept is **frailty rather than disease**. An Elysian of 115 EY
typically carries several chronic conditions, none of which is what will kill
them; what determines their trajectory is cumulative physiological reserve.
Elysian medicine measures frailty routinely from 70 EY, treats it as the primary
variable, and judges interventions by their effect on it rather than on any
single organ system. The frailty-weighted dependency ratio of 0.31
(`hist.demographics` §3) is derived from this clinical measure, not the reverse.

### Late-life cognitive decline

**The single largest cause of disability in the Concord**, affecting 21% of
Elysians over 110 EY, and the most conspicuous unsolved problem in Elysian
medicine.

Its prominence is a direct consequence of success. A civilization whose people
routinely reach 112 years encounters, at scale, a category of neurological
attrition that shorter-lived populations largely die before reaching. Two
centuries of research have produced good symptomatic management, meaningful
delay — median onset has moved from 96 EY to 108 EY — and **no prevention and no
reversal**.

It is also the field where the neural privacy prohibition
(`res.sciences` §6) bites hardest, since the correlational work that might
accelerate understanding is exactly what Charter right 7 forecloses. Practitioners
who support the prohibition and work on this disease say both things at once, and
canon records the tension without resolving it.

## 2. Mental Health

Mental health services are integrated with general practice rather than separated
from it: the named clinician holds mental and physical health in one record and
one relationship, and 61% of mental health care is delivered at Commune or
District level without specialist referral.

**Crisis response is not a police matter.** Commune Response Teams
(`law.policing` §1) attend mental health emergencies unarmed, with no power of
arrest, and 61% of all emergency calls resolve without police involvement at all.

**Involuntary treatment is narrow and judicial.** Detention for mental health
reasons requires a judicial order, is time-limited to 14 days before renewal,
carries an automatic right to counsel from the Public Legal Service, and is
reviewed by a body independent of the treating service. Charter right 5
(freedom from arbitrary detention) is non-derogable and applies. 41,000 orders
are made annually across 7.25 billion people, and every one is published in
anonymised form.

**Outcomes are honest and mixed.** Access is excellent, stigma is low by any
historical standard, and the suicide rate is 2.1 per 100,000 — but that rate has
been essentially flat for sixty years despite substantial investment, and the
Concord's mental health services describe the plateau in their own reviews as
unexplained. Elysian psychiatry does not claim to have solved this.

## 3. Substance Use

Drug use is not a crime anywhere in the Concord (`law.substantive` §2.1).
Psychoactive substances are supplied through a licensed system with quality
control, potency labelling, age limits at 16 EY, and no advertising. Supplying
outside that system is an offence; consuming is not.

**Treatment is available on demand**, without referral, without waiting, and
without any requirement to stop using first. Dependence is managed as a chronic
health condition with relapse expected and planned for.

The measured results are what the Concord cites when defending the policy:
overdose deaths are 0.9 per 100,000; contaminated-supply deaths are effectively
zero; and dependence prevalence is comparable to Integration-era levels while the
harms attached to it are a small fraction. What the policy has not done is reduce
dependence itself, and canon states that plainly rather than claiming otherwise.

## 4. Genetics and Reproduction

**Somatic gene therapy is routine** — 2.1 million procedures annually, mostly for
single-gene disorders, cancers, and immune conditions. Helicin's redundant codon
families (`planet.biosphere` §1) make targeted correction more tractable than
Earth genetics would allow.

**Heritable germline modification is prohibited** except to prevent a specific
severe disorder, under judicial licence, with the Office of Future Generations
heard as of right. Fourteen licences have been granted since EY 340. The
prohibition rests on consent: a modified descendant cannot agree to what was done
to them, and the Concord treats the narrow disease exception as the most it can
justify.

**Reproductive decisions are entirely private.** Charter right 4 forbids any
birth quota, incentive, or restriction (`gov.constitution` §3), and this is
enforced against soft pressure as well as hard: carrier screening is offered,
never recommended; no clinician may advise for or against a pregnancy on
population grounds; and no public body may collect reproductive intention data.
The Concord's demographic flatness is a consequence of freedom, not of policy,
and the constitution keeps it that way deliberately.

## 5. End of Life

With 68% of deaths following age-associated decline and lifespans of 112 years,
dying on Elysium is usually slow, anticipated, and prepared for. Palliative
medicine is a major specialty, integrated from the point of a life-limiting
diagnosis rather than at its end, and 84% of Elysians die at home or in a
Commune facility rather than in hospital.

**Medically assisted dying is lawful**, under safeguards that canon should
describe precisely because they are the substance of the arrangement:

- The person must be of legal majority, decisionally competent at every stage,
  and acting without coercion.
- A settled, repeated request across a minimum of 90 days, with a second
  independent clinical assessment and an independent non-clinical review.
- No eligibility on grounds of disability, age, dependence, or cost. Frailty
  alone does not qualify; a specific condition causing suffering the person finds
  unbearable is required.
- Any clinician may decline for any reason, without stating one, and without
  professional consequence.
- Every case is reviewed after the fact by a body independent of the treating
  service, and the anonymised record is published.

Roughly 1.4% of deaths occur this way.

**The objection is recorded in canon with full force**, because it is genuinely
held and has never been answered. Its critics — including a substantial minority
in the Assembly, several disability organisations, and most theistic traditions —
argue that no safeguard can distinguish a free choice from one made by someone
who has been made to feel like a burden, and that in a society with 710 million
very old people the pressure need never be spoken to be felt. They point out that
the Concord's own monitoring can detect coercion only when it leaves evidence.

The Concord's answer is the safeguards, the review, and the published data. Its
critics regard that answer as insufficient. Two referendum campaigns to repeal
have failed, both narrowly. **This is a live and unresolved moral disagreement in
which the Concord has chosen a side, and canon does not present the choice as
settled.**

## 6. Epidemic Response

Biosecurity is an **enumerated Concord power** (`gov.constitution` §2.1), one of
the few things the founders took upward without argument, and the reason is the
Kessander Plague: an engineered pathogen released by a non-state group that
killed 340 million people (`hist.timeline` §8).

The standing vulnerability is demographic. **A very old population is
disproportionately fragile to a novel pathogen** (`hist.demographics` §6), and
the Concord's population is far older now than it was then. Everything below is
sized against that.

| Capability | Standing level |
|---|---|
| Genomic surveillance | Continuous, 4,100 sentinel sites, wastewater and clinical |
| Vaccine platform capacity | Full planetary course within 210 days of sequence |
| Reserve isolation beds | 14 million, maintained empty |
| Antiviral and antimicrobial stockpile | 2 Elysian years of projected need |
| Containment facilities | 41 Concord facilities at the highest tier |
| Exercise cadence | Full planetary exercise every 4 years, unannounced within a stated quarter |

Emergency powers apply and are caged exactly as elsewhere: 30-day automatic
lapse, 180-day absolute cap, automatic judicial review, non-derogable rights
untouched (`gov.constitution` §6). Quarantine is possible; indefinite quarantine
is not, and the 48-hour production requirement (`law.substantive` §3) has no
epidemic exception.

**Research restraint is part of the strategy.** Enhancing pathogen
transmissibility or lethality is prohibited absolutely (`res.system` §3), and the
biosecurity establishment contains a standing minority that regards this as
leaving the Concord unprepared. That argument is live and the prohibition holds.

## 7. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **Late-life cognitive decline** | 21% of over-110s, no prevention and no reversal after two centuries, and the research that might help is foreclosed by a privacy prohibition its own practitioners support |
| **The suicide plateau** | Flat for sixty years despite investment; described by the services themselves as unexplained |
| **Dependence unchanged** | Regulated supply has near-eliminated the harms of dependence without reducing dependence |
| **Assisted dying and coercion** | Monitoring detects coercion only when it leaves evidence; the objection is unanswered and two repeal campaigns have failed narrowly |
| **Pathogen preparedness is bounded by restraint** | 210 days to a planetary vaccine course is fast and not instant, and the Concord has chosen not to study the threats it fears most |
| **Germline licences drift** | Fourteen licences in seventy years, and each has slightly widened what counts as a specific severe disorder |
| **Reserve capacity is expensive and idle** | 14 million empty beds and a two-year stockpile cost real money continuously to answer a risk that arrives rarely |
