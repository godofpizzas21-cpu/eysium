## 1. Markets Are Ordinary

The Concord is a market economy. Prices are mostly free, firms mostly private,
competition mostly real, and Elysians start businesses, fail at them, and start
others. Nothing in the founding philosophy is anti-commercial: the founders'
quarrel was with unaccountable concentration and with markets that priced
everything except the future, not with exchange.

What is unusual is the **distribution of ownership forms**, which is far more
plural than any Earth economy.

| Form | Share of employment | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Private firms | 41% | Investor-owned; the ordinary commercial sector |
| **Worker cooperatives** | 27% | Owned by their workers, one member one vote |
| Public enterprises | 14% | Publicly owned, commercially operated, at every tier |
| Consumer and producer cooperatives | 9% | Retail, agriculture, housing, utilities |
| **Stewardship foundations** | 6% | Firms owned by a purpose-bound trust with no distributable owner |
| Sole traders and partnerships | 3% | — |

**Worker cooperatives are not a fringe.** At 27% of employment they are the
second-largest form, and their prevalence is not the result of a mandate — no
law requires cooperative ownership. It follows from three ordinary rules: the
public risk-capital system lends to cooperatives on the same terms as to
investor firms, the wealth transfer tax makes selling a firm to its workers far
cheaper than bequeathing it, and cooperative conversion carries a tax deferral.
The Concord shaped a landscape and let firms choose their form on it — the same
"convergence by attraction" technique as the model codes (`law.substantive` §1).

**Stewardship foundations** hold a firm's equity in a trust bound to a stated
purpose. There is no owner who can sell, and profits either fund the purpose or
are reinvested. They are concentrated in long-horizon industries — pharmaceutical
research, seed stock, grid equipment, deep infrastructure — where a hundred-year
commitment is the product.

## 2. Enterprise and Capital

**Formation is trivial.** Registering a firm takes under an hour, is free, and
is done through the same public identity infrastructure as everything else.
Failure is equally unceremonious: personal bankruptcy discharges in 3 Elysian
years and carries no continuing disability, on the reasoning that a society that
punishes failure gets less attempt.

**Public risk capital.** Regional development banks and the Concord Research
Fund supply patient equity and long-term loans, taking minority stakes and
never control. They are required to publish their entire portfolio including
failures, and their published loss rate is 34% — a number the Concord regards as
appropriately high, since a public risk fund with a low loss rate is not taking
risk.

**Stock markets exist**, listing about 41,000 firms, and are unremarkable except
in three respects:

- **A holding-period transaction levy.** The levy on a sale falls with the
  holding period: 1.2% under a day, declining to zero above two Elysian years.
  It exists to make ultra-short trading unprofitable without prohibiting it, and
  it reduced turnover by roughly four-fifths when introduced in EY 214.
- **Long-form disclosure.** Listed firms report ecological effect, materials
  use against the Constrained List, distributional effect of pay, and a
  reversibility statement for major commitments — the same instrument required
  of legislation (`gov.institutions` §2.3).
- **No dual-class shares and no anti-takeover entrenchment.** One share, one
  vote, without exception.

## 3. Work

### Working time

The standard working week is **34 civil hours over 5 days of the 8-day week**
(`hist.calendar` §5), leaving Seldan and Ovdan as rest days and one further
weekday free by rotation.

Work is arranged around biphasic sleep (`hist.demographics` §1): a first-waking
block of about 4.5 civil hours, then **the Stillness**, then a second-waking
block of about 2.5 hours. Scheduling work during the Stillness is prohibited
except in continuous-operation and emergency services, where it is compensated
at a statutory premium and limited in frequency.

Elysians work markedly less than their productivity would permit, and this is a
choice rather than an accident. Working time fell steadily from the founding
until roughly EY 340 and has been broadly flat since; the political argument
about whether to take further gains as time or as income is a permanent feature
of Concord labour politics.

### Careers across a long life

A 60-year working life changes what employment means.

- **Median retirement is 88 EY**, but retirement is a process rather than an
  event: hours reduce progressively, and 31% of Elysians over 88 EY continue in
  some paid work.
- **The fallow entitlement.** Every worker accrues a right to a **fallow
  period** — up to one Elysian year away from work, job protected, paid at 45% —
  once every twelve years of employment. It is used for study, care, travel,
  illness, starting ventures, and doing nothing. Take-up is 64%.
- **Retraining is a right, not a programme.** Any worker may claim funded
  education at any point in life (Phase 8), and 4.1% of the workforce is in
  substantial retraining at any time.

The fallow entitlement is expensive and its defenders make no productivity
argument for it. The stated justification is that a person who will work for
sixty years should not spend all sixty of them working continuously, and that a
civilization designed to last centuries should not treat a life as an
uninterrupted input.

### Bargaining and representation

Sectoral bargaining is the norm: unions and employer associations negotiate
sector-wide agreements covering pay floors, hours, and conditions, which then
bind all firms in the sector. Union density is 61%.

Firms above 250 workers must have **one-third worker representation on their
governing board**, rising to one-half above 2,000. Cooperatives are exempt,
being worker-governed already.

Strikes are lawful, including in most public services; the exceptions are narrow
and require the striking service to maintain a judicially approved minimum. The
Concord treats a strike as information about a bargaining failure rather than as
a disorder.

## 4. The Civic Income

Charter right 11 guarantees a subsistence income "sufficient for participation
in civic life" (`gov.constitution` §3). It is delivered as the **Civic Income**:
an unconditional cash payment to every resident adult, and a smaller payment to
the guardian of every child.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Adult payment | 34% of median equivalised household income |
| Child payment | 12% of the same |
| Conditions | None |
| Withdrawal against earnings | None — it is not withdrawn as income rises |
| Recipients | All residents aged 16 EY and above |
| Cost | 11.4% of GCP |
| Delivery | Automatic, to the settlement account, monthly |

**It does not replace the other provision rights.** Healthcare, education, and
housing are guaranteed separately (Phases 8–10); the Civic Income is not
expected to purchase them. This is the design difference Elysian economists
emphasise most: a basic income that must buy healthcare is a healthcare cut.

**Effects, as measured.** Employment participation fell by 1.9 percentage points
on introduction and has not fallen further; the decline was concentrated in
second earners in caring households and in people over 80 EY. Reported financial
distress fell by two-thirds. Small-business formation rose 21%. Mediation House
caseloads in family and debt matters fell by a third.

**The unresolved argument.** A persistent minority holds that the Civic Income
suppresses wages at the bottom by subsidising low-paying employers, and the
evidence is genuinely mixed — sectoral bargaining pushes the other way and the
two effects are hard to separate. Canon records this as unsettled rather than
picking a side.

## 5. Trade and the External Sector

The Concord is a single market with no internal barriers of any kind
(`hist.demographics` §5). There is no external trading partner, so "trade" means
interregional commerce and, increasingly, exchange with off-world settlements
(`space.infrastructure`).

Interregional trade is 34% of GCP. The one economically significant flow that
crosses the planetary boundary is **orbital and Belt materials**, priced by the
Treasury and Materials portfolio against the Constrained List levy so that
off-world beryllium and platinum-group supply compete on equal terms with
terrestrial extraction rather than being subsidised into existence.

## 6. Known Weaknesses

| Weakness | Nature |
|---|---|
| **The wage-suppression question** | Whether the Civic Income subsidises low-paying employers is genuinely unresolved after two centuries of data |
| **Cooperative growth ceiling** | Worker cooperatives are stable and productive but expand more slowly and raise capital less easily; the sector's share has not grown in ninety years |
| **Fallow inequality** | Take-up is 64% overall but 79% among high earners and 41% among low earners, so an entitlement designed for everyone is used most by those who need it least |
| **Sectoral bargaining rigidity** | Sector-wide agreements protect incumbents and can slow entry by firms with genuinely different models |
| **Working-time politics** | The time-versus-income argument is permanent and neither side can win it, so the settlement drifts rather than being decided |
| **Long-horizon capital is thin outside foundations** | Stewardship foundations solve patient capital for firms that have one; ordinary firms still face investors with shorter horizons than the Concord's own appraisal rules |
