## 1. The Species

The Elysians are the native sapient species of clade Zoaea
(`planet.biosphere` §2). They evolved on Elysium, share no ancestry with any
Earth lineage, and are built from helicin like everything else alive on the
planet.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean adult height | 1.72 m |
| Mean adult mass | 68 kg |
| Body plan | Bipedal, four-limbed, **six digits per hand** |
| Sexes | Two, with a small intersex population (≈0.9%) |
| Gestation | 290 Elysian days (≈ 0.75 Elysian years) |
| Typical singleton birth rate | 96.2% of births |
| Sexual maturity | ~13 EY (≈ 14.8 Earth years) |
| Legal majority | **16 EY** (≈ 18.2 Earth years) |
| Median lifespan at birth | **112 EY** (≈ 127 Earth years) |
| Median healthy lifespan | 104 EY (≈ 118 Earth years) |
| Maximum verified lifespan | 139 EY |

**Why Elysians live so long.** The six-base helicin genome carries redundant
codon families that repair enzymes exploit, giving Elysian cells a natively
higher error-correction capacity than Earth biochemistry allows
(`planet.biosphere` §1). Somatic mutation accumulates more slowly, cancers are
comparatively rare, and senescence begins later. This is a *biological*
inheritance, not a medical achievement — Elysians in the classical era already
outlived Earth humans. What Concord medicine added (Phase 9) was closing the gap
between median and maximum, not extending the maximum itself.

A long life changes institutions. Careers routinely span 60 years; the Concord's
civil-service tenure rules, pension design, and constitutional term limits
(Phase 4) are all calibrated to a species that can plausibly hold a grudge, or a
seat, for a very long time.

### Six digits, and why the year has twelve months

Two hands of six give twelve, and Elysians counted in **duodecimal** for most of
their history. The 12-month year, the 60-minute hour, and traditional Elysian
measures of length, volume, and land all descend from base-12 counting.

At the founding, the Meridian Convention standardized **decimal** notation for
science, law, and commerce — not because duodecimal was worse, but because four
of the five industrial powers already used decimal instruments and a planetary
metrology could not afford ambiguity. Duodecimal survives in vernacular speech,
traditional trades, music, and the calendar itself. Elysians still say "a dozen"
the way Earth speakers say "a hundred", and children learn both.

*(Per `charter.canonical-units` §1, this Bible presents all quantities in
decimal SI regardless.)*

### Sleep and the long day

The 25.9-hour day (`planet.physical`) produced a **biphasic** sleep architecture.
Elysians sleep roughly 7 civil hours at night and take a second consolidated rest
of about 1.5 civil hours in the early afternoon. This is not a cultural habit but
a circadian one: the Elysian body clock has a natural period near 26 hours and a
pronounced post-midday dip.

The consequences reach across the Bible. The Elysian working day is split rather
than continuous (Phase 6). Cities are designed with a quiet interval built into
their rhythm (Phase 10). Shift work that violates the biphasic pattern is
regulated as a health hazard (Phase 9). And the long Elysian evening — the
stretch of waking hours after the second sleep and before night — is where a
remarkable amount of Elysian social and cultural life happens (Phase 14).

## 2. Population

**Total population: 7.25 billion**, of whom 28 million live off-world
(Kalyra, orbital habitats, and Tyrran Belt stations — `space.infrastructure`).

| Region | Population | Share | Density (per km² of land) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elandris | 2.31 bn | 31.9% | 74.5 |
| Meridia | 2.18 bn | 30.1% | 37.5 |
| Thalassar | 1.16 bn | 16.0% | 42.3 |
| Auroria | 1.09 bn | 15.0% | 26.3 |
| Myriad Isles | 342 m | 4.7% | 95.0 |
| Veydra | 140 m | 1.9% | 7.2 |
| Off-world | 28 m | 0.4% | — |

Elandris holds the highest continental population and the Myriad Isles the
highest density, both as promised in `planet.geography`. Veydra is nearly empty
by design: it is a scientific commons (Phase 8), not a homeland.

**Urbanization: 87.4%.** Combined with a built environment occupying only 1.4%
of land (`planet.biosphere` §6), this is the Concord's central spatial fact —
a highly urban species living very lightly, because it lives densely.

### The demographic arc

Population peaked at 11.2 billion in 78 BE, fell to about 9.6 billion by the
founding through the Long Emergency's mortality and collapsed fertility, and then
declined *voluntarily* across the Concord era to roughly 7.2 billion, where it
has been effectively flat for eighty years.

Canon is precise about the cause, because it matters politically: **the decline
was not policy.** The Concord has never operated a birth quota, incentive, or
restriction of any kind, and the Charter forbids it. Fertility fell because
education, healthcare, secure housing, and long lives produced the choices they
usually produce. The current total fertility rate is **2.04**, statistically
indistinguishable from replacement, and the population is projected to remain
between 7.1 and 7.4 billion for the next two centuries.

There is a live and unresolved political argument about whether this is good.
Growth advocates argue a larger population accelerates research and settles space
faster; stability advocates argue the current figure is what the biosphere and
the Constrained List can carry indefinitely. Neither side has won, and the
Charter's neutrality means neither can impose an answer. Canon records this as an
**open disagreement**, not a settled question.

## 3. Age Structure

| Band | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–15 EY (minors) | 18.2% | Legal majority at 16 EY |
| 16–59 EY | 44.9% | Primary working and family-forming years |
| 60–99 EY | 27.1% | Most remain in full work; median retirement 88 EY |
| 100+ EY | 9.8% | 710 million Elysians; 2.1 million over 130 EY |

**Median age: 47 EY.** Old-age dependency in the Earth sense barely exists,
because health and working capacity extend so far: the Concord measures a
**frailty-weighted dependency ratio** instead, currently 0.31. Phase 16 defines
it formally; Phase 9 explains the medicine behind it.

This age structure is the single most demanding constraint on Concord public
finance (Phase 6) and healthcare (Phase 9), and it is also the reason Elysian
institutions are unusually good at long-term planning: the people making
century-scale decisions expect to live to see the results.

## 4. Households and Family

Elysian family structure is diverse and legally pluralistic. The Charter
recognises households rather than prescribing family forms.

| Household type | Share |
|---|---|
| Multi-generational (3+ generations) | 31% |
| Two-adult with children | 24% |
| Single-adult | 19% |
| Two-adult without children | 15% |
| Co-residential group (unrelated adults, formalised) | 11% |

Mean household size is 3.4. Multi-generational households are the plurality
because long lives make four living generations ordinary — a great-grandparent of
118 EY is unremarkable. This shapes housing design profoundly (Phase 10), which
in turn is why Elysian apartments are large, flexible, and built to be
subdivided and recombined over a century of occupancy.

## 5. Mobility and Migration

Free movement within the Concord is a Charter right. Roughly 4.1% of Elysians
change region in any given year, and 22% live outside the region of their birth.
There is no immigration control between regions and no concept of internal
citizenship — a Veydran researcher moving to Elandris is not a migrant in law,
merely a resident who has moved.

The Concord does not, at the reference date, have an external migration policy,
because there is no external population. Should first contact occur, the question
becomes urgent and unanswered (`dipl.external` §4).

## 6. Health, Mortality, and Failure Modes

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---|
| Infant mortality | 1.1 per 100,000 live births |
| Maternal mortality | 0.4 per 100,000 |
| Leading cause of death | Age-associated multi-system decline (68%) |
| Deaths from infectious disease | 1.9% |
| Deaths from injury and accident | 4.3% |
| Suicide | 2.1 per 100,000 per year |

Canon does not pretend these are perfect. Two demographic vulnerabilities are
recorded openly:

**A very old population is fragile to a novel pathogen.** The Kessander Plague
(`hist.timeline`) killed disproportionately among the old, and the Concord's
population is far older now than it was then. Epidemic response (Phase 9) is
sized against this, and it is the standing argument for the Concord's biosecurity
regime.

**A flat population with a long tail is slow to recover from mass casualty.** At
a TFR of 2.04 with 112-year lifespans, a demographic shock takes over a century
to work through. The Concord's resilience doctrine treats population as a slow
variable — like the ocean overturning — that must be protected because it cannot
be repaired quickly.
