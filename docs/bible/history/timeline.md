## 1. How to Read This History

Dates use **BE** (Before the Era) and **EY** (Elysian Year), counting from the
ratification of the Concord Charter on `EY-0001-M01-D01`. There is no year 0.
Dates before roughly 5,000 BE are approximate and given as round figures;
everything after the invention of writing is dated to the year.

One convention deserves stating at the outset, because it is itself a fact about
the Concord: **this history is not flattering.** The Concord's official
historiography (§13) is legally required to publish its own failures with the
same prominence as its achievements. What follows is the standard account taught
in Elysian schools, including the parts that reflect badly on the institution
teaching it.

## 2. Deep Time and the Emergence of the Elysians

The Elysians are a native sapient species of the clade Zoaea
(`planet.biosphere` §2) — bipedal, tool-using, intensely social, and, like every
other organism on the planet, built from helicin. They are not related to any
Earth lineage and share nothing with humanity but convergence. Their physiology
and life history are canonized in `hist.demographics` (Phase 3B).

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| ~2.4 M BE | Earliest stone tools, Alcyon headwaters |
| ~130,000 BE | Anatomically modern Elysians in eastern Meridia |
| ~90,000 BE | First dispersal into Elandris and Auroria |
| ~44,000 BE | Thalassar reached by coastal migration |
| ~26,000 BE | The Myriad Isles settled — the first deliberate open-ocean voyaging |
| ~9,000 BE | Veydra reached; last continent occupied |

Veydra was settled last and thinly, which is why it remained available to become
the Concord's scientific commons rather than anyone's homeland
(`planet.geography` §2.5).

## 3. The Alcyon Neolithic (11,200 BE – 5,400 BE)

Agriculture began where the geography made it nearly inevitable: the **River
Alcyon**, an exotic river running through the hyper-arid **Sirocc Basin**
(`planet.climate` §5). A predictable annual flood, a desert that killed weeds and
pests, and a monsoon-fed snowpack upstream produced the highest-surplus
agriculture on the planet — and, because the river was a single shared and
fragile thing, the earliest large-scale cooperative institutions.

Elysian civilization therefore began with **water administration**, not with
conquest. The oldest continuous institution on Elysium is not a monarchy or a
temple but the **Alcyon flow-share** — a rotating allocation of irrigation
rights, first attested about 8,600 BE and, in heavily modified form, still in
force today. Concord constitutional lawyers cite it more often than any other
precedent.

The 8-day week (`hist.calendar` §5) descends from the flow-share rotation.

## 4. Cities, Writing, and the Classical Era (5,400 BE – 620 BE)

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 5,400 BE | **Ilvaret** founded on the lower Alcyon — the first city |
| 4,900 BE | Writing: administrative tallies of flow-share allocations |
| 2,600 BE | Bronze-analogue metallurgy from Cindral ores; the **Meridian Leagues** |
| 1,900 BE | Ilvaret's collapse in the first recorded multi-year Sirocc drought |
| 1,100 BE | Classical natural philosophy; the first accurate measurement of Elysium's circumference |
| 840 BE | The **Concord of Nine Cities** — a defensive league, and the source of the word *Concord* |
| 620 BE | Movable-type printing in the Meridian Leagues |

The **Concord of Nine Cities** matters far beyond its size. It was a small,
squabbling, and ultimately failed alliance of Meridian city-states, but it
produced the political vocabulary — *concord*, *charter*, *convention*,
*audit* — that the founders would reach for 840 years later. That the modern
planetary state named itself after a failed classical league is not accidental:
it was a deliberate signal that the union was a *practice*, not a triumph.

Ilvaret's collapse is the other formative memory. A civilization that had lasted
three and a half millennia ended in eleven years of low water. Elysian culture
has never entirely stopped thinking about it.

## 5. The Long Reach (620 BE – 410 BE)

Thalassari maritime expansion, driven by the **Mistral Shelf** fisheries and
enabled by a maritime culture with the planet's oldest weather records. Within
two centuries every coast was in contact with every other, the Myriad Isles were
navigational hubs rather than isolates, and the first genuinely planetary
exchange of crops, diseases, and ideas took place.

It was not benign. The Long Reach brought epidemics to the Isles that killed an
estimated 40% of their population, and established the extractive trading
relationships that the industrial era would industrialize.

## 6. The Vail Awakening (410 BE – 300 BE)

Industrialization began in **Auroria**, and the reason was geological: the
**Vail Spine** placed iron, coal, and copper within a hundred kilometres of each
other, in a cold continent where mechanical power had obvious value
(`planet.resources` §2). Auroria industrialized first and dominated the following
century.

The Vail Awakening produced, within four generations, the steam engine, the
railway, the factory system, mass literacy, the first labour movements, and the
first sustained rise in living standards in Elysian history. It also produced
the coal economy whose consequences fill the next two sections.

## 7. The Integration (300 BE – 78 BE)

Global economic integration under Aurorian and later Elandric industrial power:
telegraphy, steamships, planetary markets, and formal and informal empire. Living
standards rose enormously and unevenly. Population climbed from 1.4 billion to
11.2 billion. Atmospheric CO₂ passed 400 ppm around 190 BE, 500 ppm around
120 BE.

The Integration is taught in Concord schools as **the era in which Elysians
learned to do everything except decide anything together**. Planetary systems —
climate, fisheries, capital, disease — became fully coupled while planetary
institutions remained voluntary, weak, and easily ignored. Every catastrophe of
the following century followed from that gap.

## 8. The Long Emergency (78 BE – 1 BE)

Not one apocalypse. A cascade, over 78 years, in which each failure reduced the
capacity to deal with the next. Roughly **610 million excess deaths**, a 19%
fall in global economic output at the trough, and the near-loss of several
irreplaceable systems.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 78 BE | **The Long Summer** begins: CO₂ passes 560 ppm, monsoon variability doubles |
| 71 BE | Stonebloom reef bleaching becomes annual; Amarant fisheries begin to fail |
| 64 BE | CO₂ peaks at **610 ppm**; +2.9 °C above pre-industrial |
| 61–54 BE | **The Phosphorus Famine** — fertiliser price collapse of supply, not demand; 210 million dead |
| 49–43 BE | **The Amarant War** — Elandris and Auroria over fisheries and shipping lanes |
| 38 BE | Veydran Ice Cap margin loss accelerates; 0.9 m sea-level rise commitment recognised |
| 31–27 BE | **The Sirocc War** — lithium and water; the shortest and most destructive of the wars |
| 24–21 BE | **The Kessander Plague** — an engineered pathogen released by a non-state group; 340 million dead |
| 19 BE | **The Cassian Incident** — an automated strategic-warning system escalates a sensor fault to launch-ready status; catastrophe averted by a single officer's refusal, over 90 seconds |
| 14 BE | The **Thalassar Accord** — first binding planetary treaty, on fisheries only, and it holds |
| 9 BE | The **Verdanne Compact** — the Isles and Meridia agree the first enforceable carbon limits |
| 4 BE | The **Meridian Convention** opens |

Four things about this period are load-bearing for everything the Concord later
became.

**The wars were resource wars, and everyone knew it.** The Amarant War was
fought over a fishery that collapsed anyway. The Sirocc War was fought over
lithium reserves that both sides could have shared at a fraction of the cost.
This is why the Concord's economic constitution treats resource access as a
planetary commons question rather than a national one (Phases 4, 6).

**The Cassian Incident is the most-taught event in Elysian history.** Ninety
seconds, one person, an automated system that was working exactly as designed.
Every principle of Concord AI governance — mandatory human veto, full audit
trails, the prohibition on automated escalation, the doctrine that a system's
correct operation is not the same as a correct outcome — descends directly from
those ninety seconds (`ai.governance`). The officer was **Duty Warden Teyra
Oskan**, and her name is taught to every Elysian child.

**The recoveries began before the founding.** The Thalassar Accord and the
Verdanne Compact both worked, narrowly and unglamorously, and demonstrated that
planetary agreement was possible on small things. The founders' argument was
never "we must unite or die"; it was "look — it works, on fish."

**Nobody was conquered.** No power won the Long Emergency. The Concord was not
built by a victor, and there was no victor to build it. This is the reason the
constitution could be genuinely decentralized: no participant entered it strong
enough to dominate the others, and every participant had just watched what
happened when planetary problems met national institutions.

## 9. The Meridian Convention and the Founding (4 BE – EY 1)

Four years, 1,100 delegates, at the **Meridian Stone** on the Alcyon estuary
(`planet.geography`). The Convention's procedural rules — published deliberation,
mandatory minority reports, no closed sessions after the first year — became the
Concord's transparency doctrine.

The **Concord Charter** was ratified by simultaneous planetary referendum and
came into force on `EY-0001-M01-D01`, at the northward equinox. Ratification
carried in every region, though narrowly in two, and the published margins have
never been quietly rounded.

The Charter's substance is owned by Phase 4. Three founding decisions belong to
history:

- **Decentralization by default.** Powers rest with regions unless the Charter
  explicitly assigns them upward — a direct response to the Integration's gap.
- **The audit principle.** Every organ of government is audited by a body it
  cannot appoint, fund, or discipline.
- **Ecological limits are constitutional, not legislative.** They cannot be
  suspended by an ordinary majority in a bad year, because a bad year is exactly
  when the temptation arrives.

## 10. The Concord Era (EY 1 – 412)

### The Consolidation (EY 1 – 58)
Institution-building, and the hardest period of the Concord's existence. Two
regions attempted withdrawal (EY 22 and EY 31); both were resolved by
negotiation and constitutional amendment rather than force, setting a precedent
that has held ever since. **The Abolition** — verified dismantling of all
strategic weapons — completed in EY 34.

### The Ecological Settlement (EY 40 – 96)
The phase-out of fossil combustion, completed in **EY 88**, and the writing of
the carbon corridor into the Charter in **EY 96**. Retained-carbon doctrine
(`planet.resources` §7) dates from this settlement. Atmospheric CO₂ peaked in the
Concord era at 505 ppm in EY 12 and did not return inside the constitutional
corridor until **EY 210** — 122 years of managed decline, which Elysian
historians consider the single greatest sustained act of collective patience in
their history.

### The Fusion Transition (EY 88 – 170)
First net-positive commercial fusion **EY 103**; grid transition complete
**EY 158**. Cheap, clean, abundant energy is the material precondition for most
of what the modern Concord does — desalination, recycling at 90%-plus recovery,
controlled-environment agriculture, and orbital access.

### The Outward Turn (EY 150 – 300)
Orbital industry, the first Kalyra installations (EY 194), and the beginnings of
the space infrastructure canonized in `space.infrastructure`. Driven less by exploration than by
the **Constrained List** (`planet.resources` §9): beryllium and platinum-group
scarcity made off-world sourcing an economic argument before it was a romantic
one.

### The Long Maintenance (EY 300 – present)
The current era, and deliberately unheroic. No founding, no transition, no
war — a civilization whose central project is keeping itself excellent. It is
also the era of the Concord's most instructive failures:

- **The Corran Scandal (EY 187)** — a regional procurement network captured its
  own auditors for eleven years. Detected by a data-transparency requirement its
  architects had opposed. Produced the modern Audit Service.
- **The Emergency of EY 233** — a genuine constitutional crisis in which
  emergency powers invoked for an Elandric cyclone season were extended twice
  beyond their legal basis. Ended by judicial order and the resignation of the
  executive. Produced the automatic-sunset rules on emergency powers (Phase 4).
- **The Serrance Storm Failure (EY 341)** — 4,100 dead in a cyclone the
  forecasting system predicted correctly and the evacuation system did not act
  on. Produced the current disaster doctrine (Phase 12).

## 11. The Present: EY 412

The Concord is 412 years old. It is not finished, does not consider itself
finished, and its own official history describes it as "a working arrangement
that has not yet failed." The reference date for the entire Civilization Bible
is **EY 412, Calenth 16**.

## 13. Historiography

The Concord's **Public Record Act** requires that state-published history
include failures, dissents, and the published minority reports of every major
decision, and forbids the state from operating a monopoly on historical
interpretation. School curricula are set regionally; the Cassian Incident, the
Corran Scandal, and the Emergency of EY 233 appear in all of them.

This is not modesty. It is a resilience mechanism: a civilization that hides its
failures loses the ability to detect the next one. The doctrine is stated in the
Charter and elaborated in Phases 4 and 8.
